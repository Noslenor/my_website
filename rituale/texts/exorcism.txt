# exorcism-rules | De exorcizandis obsessis a dæmonio | On Exorcizing Those Possessed by the Devil | Exorcism | Tit. XI, cap. 1
n: Solemn exorcism of the possessed may be performed only by a priest with the express permission of the local Ordinary (CIC 1917 can. 1151; CIC 1983 can. 1172).
r: 1. Sacerdos, de peculiari et expressa Ordinarii licentia, vexatos a dæmone exorcizaturus, ea qua par est pietate, prudentia, ac vitæ integritate præditus esse debet: qui non sua, sed divina fretus virtute, ab omni rerum humanarum cupiditate alienus, tam pium opus ex caritate constanter et humiliter exsequatur. Hunc præterea maturæ ætatis esse decet, et non solum officio, sed etiam morum gravitate reverendum. || 1. The priest who, with the particular and express permission of the Ordinary, is about to exorcize those troubled by the demon must be endowed with the piety, prudence and integrity of life that befit the task. Relying not on his own strength but on the power of God, and free from all desire for human things, he should carry out so holy a work steadfastly and humbly, out of charity. It is fitting, moreover, that he be of mature age, and worthy of respect not only for his office but also for the seriousness of his conduct.
r: 2. Ut igitur suo munere recte fungatur, cum alia multa sibi utilia documenta, quæ brevitatis gratia hoc loco prætermittuntur, ex probatis auctoribus, et ex usu noscere studeat; tum hæc pauca magis necessaria diligenter observabit. || 2. That he may rightly discharge his office, let him take care to learn from approved authors and from experience the many other useful instructions which, for the sake of brevity, are omitted here; and let him diligently observe these few more necessary points.
r: 3. In primis, ne facile credat, aliquem a dæmonio obsessum esse, sed nota habeat ea signa, quibus obsessus dignoscitur ab iis, qui vel atra bile, vel morbo aliquo laborant. Signa autem obsidentis dæmonis sunt: ignota lingua loqui pluribus verbis, vel loquentem intellegere; distantia, et occulta patefacere; vires supra ætatis seu condicionis naturam ostendere; et id genus alia, quæ cum plurima concurrunt, majora sunt indicia. || 3. First of all, let him not readily believe that anyone is possessed by the demon, but let him be well acquainted with the signs by which a possessed person is distinguished from those who suffer from melancholy or some other illness. The signs of a possessing demon are these: speaking at some length in an unknown tongue, or understanding one who speaks it; revealing distant or hidden things; displaying strength beyond what is natural to one's age or condition; and other things of the kind. When several of these concur, the indications are the stronger.
r: 4. Hæc autem ut magis cognoscat, post unum aut alterum exorcismum interroget obsessum, quid senserit in animo, vel in corpore, ut sciat etiam, ad quænam verba magis diaboli conturbentur, ut ea deinceps magis inculcet ac repetat. || 4. To understand these things better, after one or two exorcisms let him ask the possessed person what he experienced in mind or body, so that he may also learn at which words the devils are most disturbed, and may thereafter press and repeat those words the more.
r: 5. Advertat, quibus artibus ac deceptionibus utantur dæmones ad Exorcistam decipiendum: solent enim ut plurimum fallaciter respondere, et difficile se manifestare, ut Exorcista diu defatigatus desistat; aut infirmus videatur non esse a dæmonio vexatus. || 5. Let him be aware of the arts and deceits the demons use to mislead the exorcist. For the most part they answer falsely and reveal themselves only with difficulty, so that the exorcist, worn out at length, may give up; or so that the sick person may appear not to be troubled by the demon at all.
r: 6. Aliquando postquam sunt manifesti, abscondunt se, et relinquunt corpus quasi liberum ab omni molestia, ut infirmus putet se omnino esse liberatum: sed cessare non debet Exorcista, donec viderit signa liberationis. || 6. Sometimes, after they have shown themselves, they hide and leave the body as if free of all disturbance, so that the sick person thinks himself completely delivered. But the exorcist must not stop until he sees the signs of deliverance.
r: 7. Aliquando etiam dæmones ponunt quæcumque possunt impedimenta, ne infirmus se subjiciat exorcismis, vel conantur persuadere infirmitatem esse naturalem; interdum, durante exorcismo, faciunt ut dormiat infirmus, et ei visionem aliquam ostendunt, subtrahendo se, ut infirmus liberatus videatur. || 7. Sometimes too the demons put every obstacle they can in the way, so that the sick person will not submit to the exorcisms; or they try to persuade him that his affliction is natural. At times, during the exorcism, they make the sick person fall asleep and show him some vision, withdrawing themselves so that he seems to be delivered.
r: 8. Aliqui ostendunt factum maleficium, et a quibus sit factum, et modum ad illud dissipandum: sed caveat, ne ob hoc ad magos, vel ad sagas, vel ad alios, quam ad Ecclesiæ ministros confugiat, aut ulla superstitione, aut alio modo illicito utatur. || 8. Some reveal a spell that has been cast, by whom it was cast, and the way to undo it. But let the exorcist take care that the afflicted person does not on this account have recourse to sorcerers, witches, or anyone other than the ministers of the Church, nor make use of any superstition or other unlawful means.
r: 9. Quandoque diabolus infirmum quiescere, et suscipere sanctissimam Eucharistiam permittit, ut discessisse videatur. Denique innumerabiles sunt artes et fraudes diaboli ad decipiendum hominem, quibus ne fallatur, Exorcista cautus esse debet. || 9. Sometimes the devil lets the sick person rest and receive the Most Holy Eucharist, so that he may seem to have departed. In short, the arts and frauds of the devil for deceiving man are numberless, and the exorcist must be on his guard so as not to be deceived by them.
r: 10. Quare memor, Dominum nostrum dixisse, genus esse dæmoniorum, quod non ejicitur nisi per orationem et jejunium (Matth. 17, 20), hæc duo potissimum remedia ad impetrandum divinum auxilium, dæmonesque pellendos, exemplo Sanctorum Patrum, quoad ejus fieri poterit, tum per se, tum per alios curet adhiberi. || 10. Mindful, therefore, that our Lord said there is a kind of demon that is not cast out except by prayer and fasting (Matt. 17:20), let him take care, after the example of the holy Fathers, that these two remedies above all be employed, as far as possible, both by himself and by others, to obtain God's help and to drive out the demons.
r: 11. In ecclesia, si commode fieri potest, vel in alio religioso et honesto loco seorsum a multitudine perductus energumenus exorcizetur; sed si sit ægrotus, vel alia honesta de causa, in domo privata exorcizari poterit. || 11. The person possessed should be brought into a church, if this can conveniently be done, or into some other religious and fitting place apart from the crowd, and exorcized there. But if he is ill, or for some other good reason, he may be exorcized in a private house.
r: 12. Admoneatur obsessus, si mente et corpore valeat, ut pro se oret Deum, ac jejunet, et sacra confessione et communione sæpius ad arbitrium Sacerdotis se muniat; et dum exorcizatur, totum se colligat, et ad Deum convertat ac firma fide salutem ab eo deposcat cum omni humilitate. Et cum vehementius vexatur, patienter sustineat, nihil diffidens de auxilio Dei. || 12. If he is sound in mind and body, the possessed person should be urged to pray to God for himself, to fast, and to fortify himself frequently with holy confession and communion, as the priest judges best; and while he is being exorcized, to recollect himself entirely, turn to God, and with firm faith and all humility ask Him for deliverance. And when he is tormented more violently, let him bear it patiently, never losing trust in God's help.
r: 13. Habeat præ manibus, vel in conspectu Crucifixum. Reliquiæ quoque Sanctorum, ubi haberi possint, decenter ac tuto colligatæ, et coopertæ, ad pectus, vel ad caput obsessi reverenter admoveantur; sed caveatur, ne res sacræ indigne tractentur, aut illis a dæmone ulla fiat injuria. Sanctissima vero Eucharistia super caput obsessi, aut aliter ejus corpori ne admoveatur, ob irreverentiæ periculum. || 13. Let him hold a crucifix in his hands or have one in view. Relics of the Saints too, where they can be had, securely and decently fastened and covered, may be reverently applied to the breast or head of the possessed person; but care must be taken that sacred things are not treated unworthily and that no injury is done to them by the demon. The Most Holy Eucharist, however, is not to be placed on the head of the possessed person or otherwise applied to his body, because of the danger of irreverence.
r: 14. Exorcista ne vagetur in multiloquio, aut supervacaneis vel curiosis interrogationibus, præsertim de rebus futuris et occultis, ad suum munus non pertinentibus; sed jubeat immundum spiritum tacere, et ad interrogata tantum respondere; neve ei credatur, si dæmon simularet se esse animam alicujus Sancti, vel defuncti, vel Angelum bonum. || 14. The exorcist should not wander into much talk or into needless or curious questions, especially about future and hidden things that do not concern his office; rather, let him command the unclean spirit to be silent and to answer only what is asked. Nor is the demon to be believed if he pretends to be the soul of some Saint or of a dead person, or a good Angel.
r: 15. Necessariæ vero interrogationes sunt, ex. gr. de numero et nomine spirituum obsidentium, de tempore quo ingressi sunt, de causa, et aliis hujusmodi. Ceteras autem dæmonis nugas, risus, et ineptias Exorcista cohibeat, aut contemnat, et circumstantes, qui pauci esse debent, admoneat, ne hæc curent, neque ipsi interrogent obsessum; sed potius humiliter et enixe Deum pro eo precentur. || 15. The necessary questions are, for example, about the number and names of the possessing spirits, the time when they entered, the cause, and other such matters. All the other trifling, laughter and nonsense of the demon the exorcist should check or ignore; and he should warn the bystanders, who ought to be few, to pay no attention to these things and not to question the possessed person themselves, but rather to pray to God for him humbly and earnestly.
r: 16. Exorcismos vero faciat ac legat cum imperio, et auctoritate, magna fide, et humilitate, atque fervore; et cum viderit spiritum valde torqueri, tunc magis instet et urgeat. Et quoties viderit obsessum in aliqua corporis parte commoveri, aut pungi, aut tumorem alicubi apparere, ibi faciat signum crucis, et aqua benedicta aspergat, quam tunc in promptu habeat. || 16. Let him perform and read the exorcisms with command and authority, with great faith, humility and fervor; and when he sees the spirit greatly tormented, let him then press and insist the more. And whenever he sees the possessed person agitated or pricked in some part of the body, or a swelling appear somewhere, let him make the sign of the cross there and sprinkle it with holy water, which he should have ready at hand.
r: 17. Observet etiam ad quæ verba dæmones magis contremiscant, et ea sæpius repetat; et quando pervenerit ad comminationem, eam iterum et sæpius proferat, semper pœnam augendo; ac si videat se proficere, in ipsa perseveret per duas, tres, quatuor horas, et amplius prout poterit, donec victoriam consequatur. || 17. Let him also note at which words the demons tremble most, and repeat them often; and when he comes to the threat, let him utter it again and again, always increasing the punishment. If he sees that he is making progress, let him persevere in it for two, three, four hours, and longer as he is able, until he obtains the victory.
r: 18. Caveat proinde Exorcista, ne ullam medicinam infirmo obsesso præbeat, aut suadeat; sed hanc curam medicis relinquat. || 18. The exorcist should accordingly take care not to give or recommend any medicine to the sick person who is possessed, but leave this concern to physicians.
r: 19. Mulierem exorcizans, semper secum habeat honestas personas, quæ obsessam teneant, dum exagitatur a dæmonio; quæ quidem personæ sint patienti, si fieri potest, cognatione proximæ; atque honestatis memor Exorcista caveat, ne quid dicat, vel faciat, quod sibi, aut aliis occasio esse possit pravæ cogitationis. || 19. When exorcizing a woman, he should always have with him persons of good repute who may hold the possessed woman while she is being shaken by the demon; these persons should, if possible, be close relatives of the sufferer. And, mindful of propriety, the exorcist should take care to say or do nothing that might be an occasion of evil thoughts to himself or to others.
r: 20. Dum exorcizat, utatur sacræ Scripturæ verbis potius, quam suis, aut alienis. Jubeatque dæmonem dicere, an detineatur in illo corpore ob aliquam operam magicam, aut malefica signa, vel instrumenta, quæ si obsessus ore sumpserit, evomat; vel si alibi extra corpus fuerint, ea revelet; et inventa comburantur. Moneatur etiam obsessus, ut tentationes suas omnes Exorcistæ patefaciat. || 20. While exorcizing, let him use the words of Holy Scripture rather than his own or another's. And let him command the demon to say whether he is held in that body by some work of magic, or by charms or instruments of sorcery; if the possessed person has swallowed them, let him vomit them up; if they are elsewhere, outside the body, let the demon reveal them; and when found, let them be burned. The possessed person should also be urged to make known all his temptations to the exorcist.
r: 21. Si vero obsessus liberatus fuerit, moneatur, ut diligenter sibi caveat a peccatis, ne occasionem dæmoni præbeat in ipsum revertendi, ne fiant novissima hominis illius pejora prioribus. || 21. If the possessed person is delivered, let him be admonished to guard himself carefully against sin, so as to give the demon no occasion of returning to him, lest the last state of that man become worse than the first.

# exorcism-rite | Ritus exorcizandi obsessos a dæmonio | Rite for Exorcizing Those Possessed by the Devil | Exorcism | Tit. XI, cap. 2
r: 1. Sacerdos ab Ordinario delegatus, rite confessus, aut saltem corde peccata sua detestans, peracto, si commode fieri possit, Sanctissimo Missæ sacrificio, divinoque auxilio piis precibus implorato, superpelliceo et stola violacea indutus, et coram se habens obsessum ligatum, si sit periculum, eum, se, et astantes communiat signo crucis, et aspergat aqua benedicta, et genibus flexis, aliis respondentibus, dicat Litanias ordinarias (pag. 104) usque ad Preces exclusive. Postea dicat: || 1. The priest delegated by the Ordinary, having duly made his confession, or at least detesting his sins in his heart, and having offered the Most Holy Sacrifice of the Mass if this can conveniently be done, and having implored God's help with devout prayers, vested in surplice and violet stole, and having before him the possessed person, bound if there is danger, signs him, himself and the bystanders with the sign of the cross and sprinkles them with holy water; then, kneeling, with the others responding, he says the ordinary Litany (of the Saints, p. 104) up to the Prayers, exclusively. Afterwards he says:

r: 2. Deinde præcipiat dæmoni hunc in modum: || 2. Then he commands the demon in this manner:
t: Præcípio tibi, quicúmque es, spíritus immúnde, et ómnibus sóciis tuis hunc Dei fámulum (hanc Dei fámulam) obsidéntibus: ut per mystéria incarnatiónis, passiónis, resurrectiónis et ascensiónis Dómini nostri Jesu Christi, per missiónem Spíritus Sancti, et per advéntum ejúsdem Dómini nostri ad judícium, dicas mihi nomen tuum, diem, et horam éxitus tui, cum áliquo signo: et ut mihi Dei minístro licet indígno, prorsus in ómnibus obœ́dias: neque hanc creatúram Dei, vel circumstántes, aut eórum bona ullo modo offéndas. || I command you, unclean spirit, whoever you are, and all your companions who possess this servant (handmaid) of God: by the mysteries of the incarnation, passion, resurrection and ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ, by the sending of the Holy Spirit, and by the coming of the same our Lord to judgment, tell me your name, and the day and hour of your departure, with some sign; and obey me, a minister of God though unworthy, entirely and in all things; and do no harm in any way to this creature of God, or to the bystanders, or to their goods.
r: 3. Deinde legantur super obsessum hæc Evangelia, vel saltem unum. || 3. Then these Gospels, or at least one of them, are read over the possessed person.

Omnia per ipsum facta sunt: et sine ipso factum est nihil, quod factum est: in ipso vita erat, et vita erat lux hóminum: et lux in ténebris lucet, et ténebræ eam non comprehendérunt. Non erat ille lux, sed ut testimónium perhibéret de lúmine. Erat lux vera, quæ illúminat omnem hóminem veniéntem in hunc mundum. He came for testimony, to bear witness to the light, that all might believe through him. He was not the light, but came to bear witness to the light. That was the true light, which gives light to every man coming into this world. He was in the world, and the world was made through Him, and the world did not know Him. He came to what was His own, and His own did not receive Him. But to all who received Him He gave power to become children of God, to those who believe in His name, who were born not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God. *(Here all genuflect)* And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us; and we beheld His glory, glory as of the Only-begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth.

r: Sequens exorcismus recitari potest ab Episcopis, nec non a Sacerdotibus, qui ab Ordinariis suis ad id auctoritatem habeant. || The following exorcism may be recited by bishops, and also by priests who have authority for this from their Ordinaries.

h: Ad S. Michaelem Archangelum. Precatio || Prayer to Saint Michael the Archangel
t: Princeps gloriosíssime cæléstis milítiæ, sancte Míchael Archángele, defénde nos in prœlio advérsus príncipes et potestátes, advérsus mundi rectóres tenebrárum harum, contra spirituália nequítiæ, in cæléstibus. Veni in auxílium hóminum; quos Deus ad imáginem similitúdinis suæ fecit, et a tyránnide diáboli emit prétio magno. Te custódem et patrónum sancta venerátur Ecclésia; tibi trádidit Dóminus ánimas redemptórum in supérna felicitáte locándas. Deprecáre Deum pacis, ut cónterat sátanam sub pédibus nostris, ne ultra váleat captívos tenére hómines, et Ecclésiæ nocére. Offer nostras preces in conspéctu Altíssimi, ut cito antícipent nos misericórdiæ Dómini, et apprehéndas dracónem, serpéntem antíquum, qui est diábolus et sátanas, et ligátum mittas in abýssum, ut non sedúcat ámplius gentes. || Most glorious Prince of the heavenly host, holy Michael the Archangel, defend us in battle against the principalities and powers, against the rulers of the world of this darkness, against the spirits of wickedness in the high places. Come to the aid of men, whom God made in the image of His own likeness and bought at a great price from the tyranny of the devil. Holy Church venerates you as her guardian and patron; to you the Lord has entrusted the souls of the redeemed, to be led into heavenly bliss. Pray to the God of peace to crush Satan under our feet, so that he may no longer hold men captive and do harm to the Church. Offer our prayers in the sight of the Most High, that the mercies of the Lord may quickly come to meet us; and seize the dragon, the ancient serpent, who is the devil and Satan, and cast him bound into the abyss, so that he may no longer lead the nations astray.

Amen. || God of heaven, God of earth, God of the Angels, God of the Archangels, God of the Patriarchs, God of the Prophets, God of the Apostles, God of the Martyrs, God of the Confessors, God of the Virgins, God who have power to give life after death and rest after toil: for there is no God besides You, nor can there be any but You, Creator of all things visible and invisible, of whose kingdom there shall be no end: we humbly entreat Your glorious majesty that You would deign to deliver us mightily from every power, snare, deceit and wickedness of the infernal spirits, and to keep us safe. Through Christ our Lord. ℟. Amen.
v: Ab insídiis diáboli, líbera nos, Dómine. || From the snares of the devil, deliver us, O Lord.
v: Ut Ecclésiam tuam secúra tibi fácias libertáte servíre, te rogámus, audi nos. || That You would grant Your Church to serve You in secure freedom, we beseech You, hear us.
v: Ut inimícos sanctæ Ecclésiæ humiliáre dignéris, te rogámus, audi nos. || That You would deign to humble the enemies of holy Church, we beseech You, hear us.
r: Et aspergatur locus aqua benedicta. || And the place is sprinkled with holy water.
