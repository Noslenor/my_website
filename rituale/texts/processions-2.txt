# procession-storm | De Processione ad Repellendam Tempestatem | Procession to Ward Off a Storm | Processions | Tit. IX, cap. 8
r: Pulsantur campanæ, et iis qui adesse possunt, in Ecclesia convocatis, dicuntur Litaniæ (pag. 104), in quibus bis dicitur: || The bells are rung, and when those who are able to be present have gathered in the church, the Litany of the Saints is said (see the Litany of the Saints), in which the following is said twice:
t: A fúlgure et tempestáte, líbera nos, Dómine. || From lightning and tempest, deliver us, O Lord.
r: In fine autem dicitur: || At the end is said:
t: Pater noster *(secreto usque ad)* || Our Father *(silently until)*
v: Et ne nos indúcas in tentatiónem. || And lead us not into temptation.
x: Sed líbera nos a malo. || But deliver us from evil.
h: Psalmus 147 || Psalm 147
t: Lauda, Jerúsalem, Dóminum: * lauda Deum tuum, Sion. || Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem; * praise your God, O Sion.
t: Quóniam confortávit seras portárum tuárum: * benedíxit fíliis tuis in te. || For He has strengthened the bolts of your gates; * He has blessed your children within you.
t: Qui pósuit fines tuos pacem: * et ádipe fruménti sátiat te. || He has set peace in your borders, * and fills you with the finest of wheat.
t: Qui emíttit elóquium suum terræ: * velóciter currit sermo ejus. || He sends forth His word to the earth; * His command runs swiftly.
t: Qui dat nivem sicut lanam: * nébulam sicut cínerem spargit. || He gives snow like wool; * He scatters mist like ashes.
t: Mittit crystállum suam sicut buccéllas: * ante fáciem frígoris ejus quis sustinébit? || He sends His ice like morsels; * who can stand before His cold?
t: Emíttet verbum suum, et liquefáciet ea: * flabit spíritus ejus, et fluent aquæ. || He will send forth His word and melt them; * His wind will blow, and the waters will flow.
t: Qui annúntiat verbum suum Jacob: * justítias, et judícia sua Israël. || He declares His word to Jacob, * His statutes and His judgments to Israel.
t: Non fecit táliter omni natióni: * et judícia sua non manifestávit eis. || He has not done so for any other nation, * nor has He made His judgments known to them.

x: Qui fecit cælum et terram. || Who made heaven and earth.

h: Oratio || Prayer
t: Deus, qui culpa offénderis, pæniténtia placáris: preces pópuli tui supplicántis propítius réspice; et flagélla tuæ iracúndiæ, quæ pro peccátis nostris merémur, avérte. || O God, who are offended by sin and appeased by penance, look graciously on the prayers of Your people who make supplication to You, and turn away the scourges of Your anger, which we deserve for our sins.
t: A domo tua, quǽsumus, Dómine, spiritáles nequítiæ repellántur: et aëriárum discédat malígnitas tempestátum. || We beseech You, O Lord, let the spirits of wickedness be driven from Your house, and let the malice of the storms of the air depart.
t: Omnípotens sempitérne Deus, parce metuéntibus, propitiáre supplícibus: ut post nóxios ignes núbium, et vim procellárum in matériam tránseat laudis comminátio tempestátum. || Almighty, everlasting God, spare those who fear, be merciful to those who entreat You, that after the harmful fires of the clouds and the violence of the tempests, the threat of the storms may be turned into an occasion of praise.
t: Dómine Jesu, qui imperásti ventis et mari, et facta fuit tranquíllitas magna: exáudi preces famíliæ tuæ, et præsta; ut hoc signo sanctæ cru✠cis omnis discédat sævítia tempestátum. || Lord Jesus, who commanded the winds and the sea, and there came a great calm, hear the prayers of Your household, and grant that by this sign of the holy ✠ cross all the fury of the storms may depart.
t: Omnípotens et miséricors Deus, qui nos et castigándo sanas, et ignoscéndo consérvas: præsta supplícibus tuis; ut et tranquillitátibus hujus optátæ consolatiónis lætémur, et dono tuæ pietátis semper utámur. Per Dóminum nostrum Jesum Christum, etc. ℟. Amen. || Almighty and merciful God, who both heal us by chastising and preserve us by pardoning, grant to Your suppliants that we may rejoice in the calm of this longed-for consolation and may ever make use of the gift of Your loving kindness. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, etc. ℟. Amen.
r: Aspergatur aqua benedicta. || Holy water is sprinkled.

# procession-famine | De Processione Tempore Penuriæ et Famis | Procession in Time of Scarcity and Famine | Processions | Tit. IX, cap. 9
r: Aguntur omnia, ut supra in Litaniis majoribus (pag. 296), usque ad finem Litaniarum, in quibus bis dicitur: || Everything is done as above in the Procession on the Greater Litanies (Tit. IX, cap. 4), until the end of the Litany of the Saints, in which the following is said twice:
t: Ut fructus terræ dare, et conserváre dignéris: Te rogámus, audi nos. || That You would deign to give and preserve the fruits of the earth, we beseech You, hear us.
r: In fine autem dicitur: || At the end is said:
t: Pater noster *(secreto usque ad)* || Our Father *(silently until)*
v: Et ne nos indúcas in tentatiónem. || And lead us not into temptation.
x: Sed líbera nos a malo. || But deliver us from evil.
r: Psalmus 22 Dóminus regit me, etc., ut supra (pag. 184). In fine Glória Patri. || Psalm 22, "The Lord rules me," etc., as above. At the end, Glory be to the Father.
v: Dómine, non secúndum peccáta nostra fácias nobis. || O Lord, deal not with us according to our sins.
x: Neque secúndum iniquitátes nostras retríbuas nobis. || Nor repay us according to our iniquities.
v: Óculi ómnium in te sperant, Dómine. || The eyes of all hope in You, O Lord.
x: Et tu das illis escam in témpore opportúno. || And You give them their food in due season.
v: Meménto congregatiónis tuæ. || Remember Your congregation.
x: Quam possedísti ab inítio. || Which You have possessed from the beginning.
v: Dóminus dabit benignitátem. || The Lord will give goodness.
x: Et terra nostra dabit fructum suum. || And our land will yield its fruit.
v: Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam. || O Lord, hear my prayer.
x: Et clamor meus ad te véniat. || And let my cry come to You.
v: Dóminus vobíscum. || The Lord be with you.
x: Et cum spíritu tuo. || And with your spirit.
t: Orémus. || Let us pray.
h: Oratio || Prayer
t: Ineffábilem nobis, Dómine, misericórdiam tuam cleménter osténde: ut simul nos et a peccátis ómnibus éxuas, et a pœnis, quas pro his merémur, erípias. || Show us mercifully, O Lord, Your unspeakable mercy, that You may at once strip us of all our sins and rescue us from the punishments we deserve for them.
t: Da nobis, quǽsumus, Dómine, piæ supplicatiónis efféctum, et famem propitiátus avérte: ut mortálium corda cognóscant, et te indignánte tália flagélla prodíre, et te miseránte cessáre. || Grant us, we beseech You, O Lord, the fruit of our devout supplication, and graciously turn away famine, that the hearts of mortals may know that such scourges come forth when You are angered and cease when You show mercy.
t: Pópulum tibi súbditum pro peccátis suis fame laborántem ad te, Dómine, convérte propítius: qui quæréntibus regnum tuum ómnia adjiciénda esse dixísti: Qui vivis et regnas cum Deo Patre, etc. ℟. Amen. || O Lord, graciously turn to Yourself Your subject people, who suffer famine because of their sins, You who said that all things would be added to those who seek Your kingdom: who live and reign with God the Father, etc. ℟. Amen.

# procession-pestilence | De Processione Tempore Mortalitatis et Pestis | Procession in Time of Mortality and Pestilence | Processions | Tit. IX, cap. 10
r: Aguntur omnia, ut supra in Litaniis majoribus (pag. 296), usque ad finem Litaniarum, in quibus bis dicitur: || Everything is done as above in the Procession on the Greater Litanies (Tit. IX, cap. 4), until the end of the Litany of the Saints, in which the following is said twice:

t: Propitiáre, Dómine, supplicatiónibus nostris: et animárum et córporum medére languóribus; ut remissióne percépta, in tua semper benedictióne lætémur. || Be favorable, O Lord, to our supplications, and heal the sicknesses of our souls and bodies, that having received pardon we may ever rejoice in Your blessing.
t: Da nobis, quǽsumus, Dómine, piæ petitiónis efféctum: et pestiléntiam mortalitatémque propitiátus avérte; ut mortálium corda cognóscant, et te indignánte tália flagélla prodíre, et te miseránte cessáre. Per Dóminum nostrum Jesum Christum, etc. ℟. Amen. || Grant us, we beseech You, O Lord, the fruit of our devout petition, and graciously turn away pestilence and mortality, that the hearts of mortals may know that such scourges come forth when You are angered and cease when You show mercy. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, etc. ℟. Amen.

r: Ecclesia et viæ, per quas transeundum est, quantum fieri potest, decentius ornentur.
