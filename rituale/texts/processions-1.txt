# processions-general | De Processionibus | On Processions | Processions | Tit. IX, cap. 1
r: 1. Publicæ, sacræque Processiones, seu Supplicationes, quibus ex antiquissimo Sanctorum Patrum instituto catholica Ecclesia vel ad excitandam fidelium pietatem, vel ad commemoranda Dei beneficia, eique gratias agendas, vel ad divinum auxilium implorandum uti consuevit, qua par est religione celebrari debent: continent enim magna ac divina mysteria, et salutares christianæ pietatis fructus eas pie exsequentes a Deo consequuntur: de quibus fideles præmonere et erudire, quo tempore magis opportunum sit, Parochorum officium est. || 1. Public and sacred processions, or supplications, which the Catholic Church, by a most ancient institution of the holy Fathers, has been accustomed to use either to stir up the devotion of the faithful, or to commemorate the benefits of God and give Him thanks, or to implore the divine help, must be celebrated with fitting reverence; for they contain great and divine mysteries, and those who devoutly take part in them obtain from God the saving fruits of Christian piety. It is the duty of pastors to forewarn and instruct the faithful about these at the time when it is most opportune.
r: 2. Videant in primis Sacerdotes, aliique ecclesiastici ordinis, ut in his Processionibus ea modestia ac reverentia tum ab ipsis, tum ab aliis adhibeatur, quæ piis et religiosis hujusmodi actionibus maxime debetur. || 2. Priests and others of the clerical state should above all see to it that in these processions, both by themselves and by others, that modesty and reverence is shown which is especially due to pious and religious acts of this kind.
r: 3. Omnes decenti habitu, superpelliceis, vel aliis sacris vestibus, sine galeris, nisi pluvia cogente, induti, graviter, modeste ac devote bini suo loco procedentes, sacris precibus ita sint intenti, ut, remoto risu, mutuoque colloquio, et vago oculorum aspectu, populum etiam ad pie, devoteque precandum invitent. || 3. All, clothed in becoming dress, in surplices or other sacred vestments, without hats unless rain compels it, walking two by two in their proper places with gravity, modesty, and devotion, should be so intent on the sacred prayers that, avoiding laughter, conversation with one another, and wandering glances, they may invite the people too to pray piously and devoutly.
r: 4. Laici a Clericis, feminæ a viris separatæ, orantes prosequantur. || 4. The laity, separated from the clergy, and the women separated from the men, should follow in prayer.
r: 5. Præferatur Crux, et, ubi viget consuetudo, vexillum sacris imaginibus insignitum, non tamen factum militari seu triangulari forma. || 5. The cross is to be carried in front, and, where the custom prevails, a banner adorned with sacred images, not, however, made in military or triangular form.
r: 6. Edendi ac bibendi abusum, secumve esculenta et poculenta deferendi in sacris Processionibus, agrisque lustrandis, et suburbanis ecclesiis visitandis, tollere Parochi studeant; ac fideles, præsertim die Dominica, quæ proxime Rogationes antecedit, quantum hæc dedeceat corruptela, sæpius admoneant. || 6. Pastors should strive to abolish the abuse of eating and drinking, or of carrying food and drink along, in sacred processions, in the blessing of the fields, and in visiting churches outside the town; and they should often warn the faithful, especially on the Sunday immediately before the Rogation Days, how unbecoming this corruption is.
r: 7. Processiones prius fieri debent, deinde Missa sollemniter celebrari: nisi aliter ob gravem causam interdum Ordinario vel Clero videatur. || 7. The procession should take place first, and then Mass be solemnly celebrated, unless for a grave reason the Ordinary or the clergy should at times decide otherwise.
r: 8. Processiones autem quædam sunt ordinariæ, quæ fiunt certis diebus per annum, ut in Festo Purificationis beatæ Mariæ Virginis, et in Dominica Palmarum, et in Litaniis majoribus in Festo S. Marci Evangelistæ, et in minoribus triduo Rogationum ante Ascensionem Domini, et in Festo Ssmi Corporis Christi, vel aliis diebus pro consuetudine Ecclesiarum. || 8. Some processions are ordinary, which take place on fixed days of the year, as on the feast of the Purification of the Blessed Virgin Mary, on Palm Sunday, at the Greater Litanies on the feast of St. Mark the Evangelist, at the Lesser Litanies on the three Rogation Days before the Ascension of the Lord, and on the feast of the Most Holy Body of Christ, or on other days according to the custom of churches.
r: 9. Quædam vero sunt extraordinariæ, ut quæ aliis publicis de causis in alios dies indicuntur. || 9. Others are extraordinary, such as those appointed for other days on account of other public needs.

# procession-candlemas | De Processione in Festo Purificationis B. M. V. | The Procession on the Feast of the Purification of the Blessed Virgin Mary | Processions | Tit. IX, cap. 2
r: 1. Completa benedictione, et distributione candelarum, ut in Missali Romano præscribitur, fit Processio. Et primo Celebrans imponit incensum in thuribulum; postea Diaconus vertens se ad populum, dicit: || 1. When the blessing and distribution of the candles have been completed, as prescribed in the Roman Missal, the procession takes place. First the celebrant puts incense into the thurible; then the deacon, turning to the people, says:
t: Procedámus in pace. || Let us go forth in peace.
r: Et Chorus respondet: || And the choir answers:
t: In nómine Christi. Amen. || In the name of Christ. Amen.
r: 2. Præcedit thuriferarius cum thuribulo fumigante, deinde Subdiaconus paratus, deferens Crucem, medius inter duos acolythos cum candelabris et candelis accensis: sequitur Clerus per ordinem, ultimo Celebrans cum Diacono a sinistris, omnes cum candelis accensis in manibus: et cantantur Antiphonæ sequentes: || 2. The thurifer goes first with the smoking thurible; then the subdeacon, vested, carrying the cross, between two acolytes with candlesticks and lighted candles; the clergy follow in order, and last the celebrant with the deacon on his left, all holding lighted candles in their hands; and the following antiphons are sung:
t: *Ant.* Adórna thálamum tuum, Sion, et súscipe Regem Christum: ampléctere Maríam, quæ est cæléstis porta: ipsa enim portat Regem glóriæ novi lúminis: subsístit Virgo, addúcens mánibus Fílium ante lucíferum: quem accípiens Símeon in ulnas suas, prædicávit pópulis Dóminum eum esse vitæ et mortis, et Salvatórem mundi. || *Ant.* Adorn your bridal chamber, O Sion, and receive Christ the King; embrace Mary, who is the gate of heaven, for she carries the King of glory of the new light. The Virgin stands still, bringing in her hands the Son begotten before the day star; and Simeon, taking Him into his arms, proclaimed to the peoples that He is the Lord of life and death and the Savior of the world.
r: Alia || Another
t: *Ant.* Respónsum accépit Símeon a Spíritu Sancto, non visúrum se mortem, nisi vidéret Christum Dómini: et cum indúcerent púerum in templum, accépit eum in ulnas suas, et benedíxit Deum, et dixit: Nunc dimíttis, Dómine, servum tuum in pace. || *Ant.* Simeon had received an answer from the Holy Spirit that he would not see death until he had seen the Christ of the Lord; and when they brought the Child into the temple, he took Him into his arms, and blessed God, and said: Now You dismiss Your servant, O Lord, in peace.
r: Et ingrediendo ecclesiam, cantatur: || And on entering the church, this is sung:
t: *Resp.* Obtulérunt pro eo Dómino par túrturum, aut duos pullos columbárum: * Sicut scriptum est in lege Dómini. || *Resp.* They offered for Him to the Lord a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons, * As it is written in the law of the Lord.
v: Postquam autem impléti sunt dies purgatiónis Maríæ, secúndum legem Móysi, tulérunt Jesum in Jerúsalem, ut sísterent eum Dómino. * Sicut. || And when the days of Mary's purification were fulfilled according to the law of Moses, they brought Jesus to Jerusalem to present Him to the Lord. * As it is written.
v: Glória Patri, et Fílio, et Spirítui Sancto. * Sicut. || Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. * As it is written.
r: 3. Finita Processione, Celebrans et Ministri, depositis violaceis, accipiunt paramenta alba pro Missa. Et candelæ tenentur in manibus accensæ, dum legitur Evangelium, et iterum ab incepto Canone usque ad expletam Communionem. || 3. When the procession is finished, the celebrant and ministers lay aside the violet vestments and put on white vestments for Mass. The candles are held lighted in the hand while the Gospel is read, and again from the beginning of the Canon until the Communion is completed.

# procession-palms | De Processione in die Palmarum | The Procession on Palm Sunday | Processions | Tit. IX, cap. 3
r: 1. In Dominica Palmarum, facta benedictione, et distributione palmarum seu olivarum, post ultimam Orationem Omnipotens, etc., fit Processio; ac primo Celebrans imponit incensum in thuribulum, et Diaconus vertens se ad populum, dicit: || 1. On Palm Sunday, after the blessing and distribution of the palms or olive branches, following the last prayer, "Almighty," etc., the procession takes place. First the celebrant puts incense into the thurible, and the deacon, turning to the people, says:
t: Procedámus in pace. || Let us go forth in peace.
r: Et Chorus respondet: || And the choir answers:
t: In nómine Christi. Amen. || In the name of Christ. Amen.
r: 2. Præcedit thuriferarius cum thuribulo fumigante; deinde Subdiaconus paratus, deferens Crucem, medius inter duos acolythos cum candelabris et candelis accensis: sequitur Clerus per ordinem, ultimo Celebrans cum Diacono a sinistris, omnes cum ramis in manibus: et cantantur sequentes Antiphonæ, vel omnes, vel aliquæ, quousque durat Processio. || 2. The thurifer goes first with the smoking thurible; then the subdeacon, vested, carrying the cross, between two acolytes with candlesticks and lighted candles; the clergy follow in order, and last the celebrant with the deacon on his left, all holding branches in their hands; and the following antiphons are sung, either all or some of them, for as long as the procession lasts.
t: *Ant.* Cum appropinquáret Dóminus Jerosólymam, misit duos ex discípulis suis, dicens: Ite in castéllum, quod est contra vos: et inveniétis pullum ásinæ alligátum, super quem nullus hóminum sedit: sólvite, et addúcite mihi. Si quis vos interrogáverit, dícite: Opus Dómino est. Solvéntes adduxérunt ad Jesum: et imposuérunt illi vestiménta, et sedit super eum: álii expandébant vestiménta sua in via: álii ramos de arbóribus exsternébant: et qui sequebántur, clamábant: Hosánna, benedíctus qui venit in nómine Dómini: benedíctum regnum patris nostri David: Hosánna in excélsis: miserére nobis, fili David. || *Ant.* When the Lord drew near to Jerusalem, He sent two of His disciples, saying: Go into the village that is over against you, and you will find the colt of an ass tied, on which no man has ever sat; untie it and bring it to Me. If anyone questions you, say: The Lord has need of it. They untied it and brought it to Jesus, and laid their garments upon it, and He sat on it. Some spread their garments in the way; others strewed branches from the trees; and those who followed cried out: Hosanna! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord! Blessed is the kingdom of our father David! Hosanna in the highest! Have mercy on us, Son of David.
r: Alia || Another
t: *Ant.* Cum audísset pópulus, quia Jesus venit Jerosólymam, accepérunt ramos palmárum: et exiérunt ei óbviam, et clamábant púeri, dicéntes: Hic est qui ventúrus est in salútem pópuli. Hic est salus nostra, et redémptio Israël. Quantus est iste, cui Throni et Dominatiónes occúrrunt! Noli timére, fília Sion: ecce Rex tuus venit tibi, sedens super pullum ásinæ, sicut scriptum est. Salve, Rex, fabricátor mundi, qui venísti redímere nos. || *Ant.* When the people heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem, they took branches of palms and went out to meet Him, and the children cried out, saying: This is He who is to come for the salvation of the people. He is our salvation and the redemption of Israel. How great is He whom Thrones and Dominations go out to meet! Fear not, daughter of Sion: behold, your King comes to you, sitting on an ass's colt, as it is written. Hail, O King, Maker of the world, who have come to redeem us.
r: Alia || Another
t: *Ant.* Ante sex dies sollémnis Paschæ, quando venit Dóminus in civitátem Jerúsalem, occurrérunt ei púeri: et in mánibus portábant ramos palmárum, et clamábant voce magna, dicéntes: Hosánna in excélsis: benedíctus qui venísti in multitúdine misericórdiæ: Hosánna in excélsis. || *Ant.* Six days before the solemn feast of the Passover, when the Lord came into the city of Jerusalem, the children went out to meet Him; and in their hands they carried branches of palms, and they cried with a loud voice, saying: Hosanna in the highest! Blessed are You who have come in the abundance of Your mercy! Hosanna in the highest!
r: Alia || Another
t: *Ant.* Occúrrunt turbæ cum flóribus et palmis Redemptóri óbviam: et victóri triumphánti digna dant obséquia: Fílium Dei ore gentes prǽdicant: et in laudem Christi voces tonant per núbila: Hosánna! || *Ant.* The crowds go out with flowers and palms to meet the Redeemer, and pay worthy homage to the triumphant Victor; the nations proclaim the Son of God with their lips, and in praise of Christ their voices thunder through the clouds: Hosanna!
r: Alia || Another
t: *Ant.* Cum Angelis et púeris fidéles inveniámur, triumphatóri mortis clamántes: Hosánna in excélsis. || *Ant.* With the Angels and the children may we be found faithful, crying out to the Conqueror of death: Hosanna in the highest!
r: Alia || Another
t: *Ant.* Turba multa, quæ convénerat ad diem festum, clamábat Dómino: Benedíctus qui venit in nómine Dómini: Hosánna in excélsis. || *Ant.* A great multitude, which had gathered for the festival day, cried out to the Lord: Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest!
r: 3. In reversione Processionis, duo vel quatuor cantores intrant in ecclesiam, et clauso ostio, stantes versa facie ad Processionem, incipiunt ℣. Glória, laus, etc., et decantant duos primos Versus, Sacerdos vero, cum aliis extra ecclesiam, repetit eosdem. Deinde qui sunt intus, cantant alios Versus sequentes, vel omnes, vel partem, prout videbitur: et qui sunt extra, ad quoslibet duos Versus respondent: Glória, laus, etc., sicut a principio. || 3. On the return of the procession, two or four cantors go into the church and, the door being shut, stand facing the procession and begin the verse "Glory, praise," etc., singing the first two lines; and the priest, with the others outside the church, repeats them. Then those inside sing the following verses, either all or some of them, as seems good; and those outside answer each couplet with "Glory, praise," etc., as at the beginning.
t: Glória, laus, et honor tibi sit, Rex Christe, Redémptor: / Cui pueríle decus prompsit Hosánna pium. || Glory, praise, and honor be Yours, O Christ, King and Redeemer, / to whom the glory of children poured forth a loving Hosanna.
t: 1. Israël es tu Rex, Davídis et ínclita proles: / Nómine qui in Dómini, Rex benedícte, venis. ℟. Glória, laus. || 1. You are the King of Israel, the glorious offspring of David, / O blessed King, who come in the name of the Lord. ℟. Glory, praise.
t: 2. Cœtus in excélsis te laudat cǽlicus omnis, / Et mortális homo, et cuncta creáta simul. ℟. Glória, laus. || 2. The whole host of heaven praises You on high, / and mortal man, and all created things together. ℟. Glory, praise.
t: 3. Plebs Hebrǽa tibi cum palmis óbvia venit: / Cum prece, voto, hymnis, ádsumus ecce tibi. ℟. Glória, laus. || 3. The Hebrew people came to meet You with palms; / with prayer, with vows, with hymns, behold, we come before You. ℟. Glory, praise.
t: 4. Hi tibi passúro solvébant múnia laudis: / Nos tibi regnánti pángimus ecce melos. ℟. Glória, laus. || 4. They paid their tribute of praise to You about to suffer; / behold, we raise our song to You who reign. ℟. Glory, praise.
t: 5. Hi placuére tibi, pláceat devótio nostra: / Rex bone, Rex clemens, cui bona cuncta placent. ℟. Glória, laus. || 5. Their praise was pleasing to You; may our devotion please You, / good King, merciful King, to whom all good things are pleasing. ℟. Glory, praise.
r: 4. Postea Subdiaconus hastili Crucis percutit portam: qua statim aperta, Processio intrat Ecclesiam, cantando: || 4. Then the subdeacon strikes the door with the staff of the cross; it is opened at once, and the procession enters the church, singing:
t: *Resp.* Ingrediénte Dómino in sanctam civitátem, Hebræórum púeri, resurrectiónem vitæ pronuntiántes, * Cum ramis palmárum, Hosánna clamábant in excélsis. || *Resp.* As the Lord entered the holy city, the children of the Hebrews, proclaiming the resurrection of life, * With branches of palms cried out: Hosanna in the highest.
v: Cumque audísset pópulus, quod Jesus veníret Jerosólymam, exiérunt óbviam ei. * Cum ramis. || And when the people heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem, they went out to meet Him. * With branches.
r: Et non dicitur Glória Patri. || The "Glory be to the Father" is not said.
r: 5. Deinde celebratur Missa: et rami tenentur in manibus, dum cantantur Passio et Evangelium tantum. || 5. Then Mass is celebrated, and the branches are held in the hand only while the Passion and Gospel are sung.

# procession-litanies | De Processione in Litaniis majoribus in Festo S. Marci Evangelistæ, et in minoribus Triduo Rogationum ante Ascensionem Domini | The Procession at the Greater Litanies on the Feast of St. Mark the Evangelist, and at the Lesser Litanies on the Three Rogation Days before the Ascension of the Lord | Processions | Tit. IX, cap. 4
r: 1. Clerus et populus, hora statuta mane in ecclesia congregati, omnes contrito et humili corde Deum flexis genibus paulisper precentur. Sacerdos amictu, alba, cingulo, stola et pluviali cum Ministris, vel saltem superpelliceo et stola violacei coloris sit indutus; cujus coloris habitu in reliquis Processionibus semper utatur, præterquam in Processionibus Corporis Christi, et quæ fiunt sollemnibus diebus, vel in gratiarum actionem; quibus diebus utitur colore propriæ sollemnitati congruenti. Ceteri vero Sacerdotes et Clerici superpelliceis induti, sequentem Antiphonam stantes concinant. || 1. The clergy and people, gathered in the church in the morning at the appointed hour, all pray to God for a while on their knees with contrite and humble heart. The priest, with his ministers, is vested in amice, alb, cincture, stole, and cope, or at least in surplice and stole, of violet color; and he always uses vesture of this color in the other processions, except in the processions of Corpus Christi and those held on solemn days or in thanksgiving, on which days he uses the color proper to the solemnity. The other priests and clerics, vested in surplices, standing, sing together the following antiphon.
t: *Ant.* Exsúrge, Dómine, ádjuva nos, et líbera nos propter nomen tuum. || *Ant.* Arise, O Lord, help us, and deliver us for Your name's sake.
t: *Ps.* Deus, áuribus nostris audívimus: * patres nostri annuntiavérunt nobis. Glória Patri. || *Ps.* O God, we have heard with our ears; * our fathers have declared it to us. Glory be to the Father.
r: Et repetitur: Exsúrge, Dómine. || And "Arise, O Lord" is repeated.
r: 2. Deinde genuflectant omnes: et duo Clerici ante Altare majus genuflexi, devote Litanias Sanctorum (pag. 104) cantare incipiant, ceteris idem simul repetentibus. || 2. Then all kneel, and two clerics, kneeling before the high altar, devoutly begin to sing the Litany of the Saints (page 104), the others repeating the same together.
r: 3. Cum autem cantatum erit: Sancta María, ora pro nobis, surgunt omnes, et ordinatim procedunt, egredientes, Litaniasque prosequentes, præcedente Cruce, et sequente Clero, ultimo loco Sacerdos paratus, ut supra dictum est, cum Ministris, prout res vel locus postulat, sacris vestibus indutis. || 3. When "Holy Mary, pray for us" has been sung, all rise and go forth in order, continuing the Litany, the cross going in front and the clergy following, and in the last place the priest, vested as said above, with his ministers clothed in sacred vestments as the occasion or place requires.
n: The Litany of the Saints referred to here (page 104 of the book) is given above with the Seven Penitential Psalms.
r: 4. Si Processio sit longior, vel repetantur Litaniæ, vel Litaniis absolutis usque ad Preces exclusive, dicantur aliqui Psalmi ex Pœnitentialibus, seu Gradualibus. Hymni vero, vel cantica lætitiæ in iis, aut aliis pœnitentiæ causa institutis Processionibus, ne dicantur. || 4. If the procession is rather long, either the Litany is repeated, or, when the Litany has been finished up to the Preces exclusively, some of the Penitential or Gradual Psalms are said. But hymns or songs of rejoicing are not to be said in these processions, or in others instituted for the sake of penance.
r: 5. Si ad unam vel plures Ecclesias in via sit divertendum, ingressis Ecclesiam, intermissisque Litaniis vel Psalmis, cantatur Antiphona cum Versu et Oratione sancti Patroni illius Ecclesiæ. Deinde egredientes, resumptis Litaniis vel Psalmis, eodem ordine procedunt usque ad Ecclesiam, ubi cum Precibus et Orationibus omnibus terminatur, ut in fine Litaniarum Sanctorum (pag. 106) post septem Psalmos Pœnitentiales. || 5. If on the way they are to turn aside to one or more churches, on entering the church the Litany or psalms are interrupted, and the antiphon with the versicle and prayer of the holy patron of that church is sung. Then, going out and resuming the Litany or psalms, they proceed in the same order to the church where the procession ends with all the Preces and prayers, as at the end of the Litany of the Saints (page 106) after the seven Penitential Psalms.
r: 6. Si contingat transferri Festum S. Marci Evangelistæ, non tamen transfertur Processio, nisi quando prædictum Festum occurrat in die Paschæ: tunc enim in Feriam III sequentem transferatur. || 6. If the feast of St. Mark the Evangelist happens to be transferred, the procession is not transferred with it, except when that feast falls on Easter Sunday; then it is transferred to the following Tuesday.

# procession-corpus-christi | De Processione in Festo Ssmi Corporis Christi | The Procession on the Feast of the Most Holy Body of Christ | Processions | Tit. IX, cap. 5
r: 1. Decenter ornentur Ecclesiæ et parietes viarum, per quas est transeundum, tapetibus et aulæis, et sacris imaginibus, non autem profanis aut vanis figuris, seu indignis ornamentis. || 1. The churches, and the walls along the streets through which the procession is to pass, should be becomingly adorned with tapestries, hangings, and sacred images, but not with profane or frivolous figures or unworthy ornaments.
r: 2. Sacerdos primum Missam celebret, in qua duas hostias consecret, et sumpta una, alteram in tabernaculum in Processione deferendum ita reponat, ut per vitrum seu crystallum, quo ipsum tabernaculum circumseptum esse debet, exterius adorantibus appareat. || 2. The priest first celebrates Mass, in which he consecrates two hosts; having consumed one, he places the other in the monstrance to be carried in the procession, in such a way that it is visible from outside to those adoring, through the glass or crystal with which the monstrance must be enclosed.
r: Peracto autem Sacrificio, et Processione jam inchoata, ordine, quo jam supra dictum est in Litaniis majoribus, Sacerdos pluviali albo indutus, ter incenset Sacramentum genuflexus. || When the Sacrifice is finished, and the procession has begun in the order described above for the Greater Litanies, the priest, vested in a white cope, kneeling, incenses the Sacrament three times.
r: 3. Deinde unus e Clericis oblongum ac decens velum circumponit scapulis Sacerdotis, qui parte veli ante pectus pendenti, utraque manu cooperta, ostensorium seu tabernaculum a Diacono sibi porrectum reverenter suscipit: mox Sacramentum ante faciem tenens, vertit se ad populum; inde procedit sub umbellam comitantibus Ministris, et duo acolythi vel Clerici cum thuribulis fumantibus præcedunt. || 3. Then one of the clerics places a long and becoming veil about the priest's shoulders; with both hands covered by the part of the veil hanging in front of his breast, he reverently receives the monstrance handed to him by the deacon. Then, holding the Sacrament before his face, he turns to the people, and proceeds under the canopy accompanied by his ministers, while two acolytes or clerics with smoking thuribles go before.
