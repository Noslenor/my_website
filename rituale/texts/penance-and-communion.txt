# penance-rubrics | De Sacramento Pœniténtiæ | The Sacrament of Penance | Sacraments | Tit. III, cap. 1
r: 1. Sanctum Pœniténtiæ Sacraméntum, ad eos, qui post Baptísmum lapsi sunt, in grátiam Dei restituéndos a Christo Dómino institútum, eo diligéntius administrándum est, quo frequéntior est ejus usus, et quo plura requirúntur ad illud recte digneque tractándum ac suscipiéndum. Cum autem ad illud constituéndum tria concúrrant, matéria, forma, et miníster: illíus quidem remóta matéria sunt peccáta, próxima vero sunt actus pœniténtis, nempe contrítio, conféssio, et satisfáctio. Forma autem, illa absolutiónis verba: *Ego te absólvo, etc.* Miníster dénique est Sacérdos habens potestátem absolvéndi vel ordináriam vel delegátam. Sed in perículo mortis omnes Sacerdótes, licet ad confessiónes non approbáti, válide et lícite absólvunt quóslibet pœniténtes a quibúsvis peccátis aut censúris, quantumvis reservátis et notóriis, étiamsi præsens sit Sacérdos approbátus. In ejus minístro requíritur étiam bónitas, sciéntia, atque prudéntia, cum sigíllo secrétæ confessiónis sub exácto, perpetuóque siléntio. Quibus et áliis ad id opportúnis ut óptime sint instrúcti, omni stúdio curáre debent Confessárii. || 1. The holy Sacrament of Penance, instituted by Christ the Lord to restore to the grace of God those who have fallen after Baptism, is to be administered all the more carefully the more frequent its use, and the more things are required for treating and receiving it rightly and worthily. Since three things concur in constituting it, matter, form, and minister: its remote matter is sins, while its proximate matter is the acts of the penitent, namely contrition, confession, and satisfaction. The form is those words of absolution: *I absolve you, etc.* The minister, lastly, is a priest having the power of absolving, either ordinary or delegated. But in danger of death all priests, even though not approved for confessions, validly and licitly absolve any penitents from any sins or censures whatever, however reserved and notorious, even if an approved priest is present. In its minister there are further required goodness, knowledge, and prudence, together with the seal of secret confession under strict and perpetual silence. Confessors must take every care to be thoroughly instructed in these and in other things suited to this end.
r: 2. In primis memínerit Confessárius, se júdicis páriter et médici persónam sustinére, ac divínæ justítiæ simul et misericórdiæ minístrum a Deo constitútum esse, ut tamquam árbiter inter Deum et hómines, honóri divíno et animárum salúti consúlat. || 2. First of all, let the confessor remember that he bears the person of a judge and likewise of a physician, and that he has been appointed by God as minister both of divine justice and of divine mercy, so that, as an arbiter between God and men, he may serve the honor of God and the salvation of souls.
r: 3. Ut ergo recte judicáre queat, discérnens inter lepram et lepram, et tamquam perítus médicus animárum morbos prudénter curáre, et apta cuíque remédia applicáre sciat, quantam potest máximam ad id sciéntiam atque prudéntiam, tum assíduis ad Deum précibus, tum ex probátis auctóribus, præsértim e Catechísmo Románo, et prudénti consílio peritórum stúdeat sibi comparáre. || 3. In order, then, that he may judge rightly, distinguishing between leprosy and leprosy, and, like a skilled physician, may know how to treat the diseases of souls prudently and to apply to each the fitting remedies, let him strive to acquire for this the greatest knowledge and prudence he can, both by constant prayer to God and from approved authors, especially the Roman Catechism, and by the prudent counsel of experienced men.
r: 4. Sciat casus et censúras Sedi Apostólicæ, et Ordinário suo reservátas, et suæ cujúsque Ecclésiæ constitutiónes, easque diligénter obsérvet. || 4. Let him know the cases and censures reserved to the Apostolic See and to his own Ordinary, and the constitutions of his own Church, and let him observe them diligently.
r: 5. Dénique hujus Sacraménti doctrínam omnem recte nosse studébit, et ália ad ejus rectam administratiónem necessária. Atque in hoc ministério ita procédat, ut infra præscríbitur. || 5. Finally, he shall strive to know rightly the whole doctrine of this Sacrament, and the other things necessary for its right administration. And in this ministry let him proceed as is prescribed below.
h: Ordo ministrándi Sacraméntum Pœniténtiæ || Order of Administering the Sacrament of Penance
r: 6. Sacérdos ad audiéndam confessiónem vocátus, promptum facilémque se præbeat: ac priúsquam ad audiéndum accédat, si tempus suppetat, ad hoc ministérium recte, sanctéque obeúndum, divínum auxílium piis précibus implorábit. || 6. A priest called to hear a confession should show himself prompt and willing; and before he goes to hear it, if time allows, he shall implore God's help with devout prayers, that he may carry out this ministry rightly and holily.
r: 7. Sacramentális confessiónis próprius locus est ecclésia vel oratórium públicum aut semi-públicum. || 7. The proper place for sacramental confession is a church or a public or semi-public oratory.
r: 8. Sedes confessionális ad audiéndas mulíerum confessiónes semper collocétur in loco paténti et conspícuo, et generátim in ecclésia vel oratório público aut semi-público muliéribus destináto. Sedes confessionális crate fixa ac ténuiter perforáta inter pœniténtem et confessárium sit instrúcta. || 8. The confessional for hearing the confessions of women is always to be placed in an open and conspicuous spot, and generally in a church or in a public or semi-public oratory intended for women. The confessional is to be fitted with a fixed, finely perforated grille between the penitent and the confessor.
r: 9. Feminárum confessiónes extra sedem confessionálem ne audiántur, nisi ex causa infirmitátis aliáve veræ necessitátis et adhíbitis cautélis quas Ordinárius loci opportúnas judicáverit. Confessiónes virórum étiam in ǽdibus privátis excípere licet. || 9. The confessions of women are not to be heard outside the confessional, except by reason of illness or some other true necessity, and with the precautions which the local Ordinary judges suitable. The confessions of men may be heard even in private houses.
r: 10. Superpellíceo, et stola violácei colóris utátur, prout tempus, vel locórum feret consuetúdo. || 10. He is to use a surplice and a violet stole, as the occasion or the custom of the place allows.
r: 11. Pœnitens, si opus sit, admoneátur, ut qua decet humilitáte mentis et hábitus accédat, et flexis génibus signo crucis se múniat. || 11. The penitent, if need be, is to be admonished to approach with fitting humility of mind and bearing, and on bended knees to sign himself with the sign of the cross.
r: 12. Mox Confessárius inquírat de illíus statu (nisi áliter notus sit) et quamprídem sit conféssus, et an impósitam pœniténtiam adimpléverit; num rite atque íntegre álias conféssus fúerit; num consciéntiam suam, ut debet, prius diligénter discússerit. || 12. Then the confessor is to inquire about his state of life (unless it is otherwise known), how long it is since his last confession, and whether he has fulfilled the penance imposed; whether he made his previous confessions properly and completely; and whether he has first examined his conscience diligently, as he ought.
r: 13. Quod si pœnitens áliqua censúra, vel casu reserváto sit ligátus, a quo ipse non possit absólvere, ne absólvat, nisi prius obténta facultáte a Superióre. || 13. If the penitent is bound by some censure or reserved case from which the confessor himself cannot absolve, he is not to absolve him unless he has first obtained the faculty from the Superior.
r: 14. Si vero Confessárius, pro personárum qualitáte, cognóverit pœniténtem ignoráre christiánæ fídei rudiménta, si tempus suppetat, eum bréviter instruat de artículis fídei, et áliis ad salútem cógnitu necessáriis, et ignorántiam ejus corrípiat, illúmque admóneat, ut ea póstmodum diligéntius addíscat. || 14. If, however, the confessor perceives, according to the character of the person, that the penitent does not know the rudiments of the Christian faith, he should, if time allows, instruct him briefly in the articles of faith and the other things necessary to be known for salvation, correct his ignorance, and admonish him to learn these things more diligently afterwards.
r: 15. Tum pœnitens confessiónem generálem latína, vel vulgári lingua dicat, scílicet *Confíteor, etc.*, vel saltem utátur his verbis: *Confíteor Deo omnipoténti, et tibi, pater.* Peccáta sua éxinde confiteátur, adjuvánte, quotiescúmque opus erit, Sacerdóte: qui confiténtem non reprehéndet nisi finíta, ut dicétur, confessióne; neque interpellábit, nisi opus sit áliquid mélius intellégere; proínde fidúciam ei præbeat, et humániter súggerat, ut ómnia peccáta sua rite et íntegre confiteátur, remóta stulta illa quorúndam verecúndia, qua præpedíti, suadénte diábolo, peccáta confitéri non audent. || 15. Then the penitent is to say the general confession in Latin or in the vernacular, that is, *I confess, etc.*, or at least use these words: *I confess to almighty God, and to you, father.* After this let him confess his sins, the priest helping him whenever need arises; the priest shall not reprove him until, as will be said, the confession is finished, nor interrupt him unless he needs to understand something better. Rather, let him give the penitent confidence and kindly encourage him to confess all his sins properly and completely, setting aside that foolish shame by which some, at the devil's prompting, are held back and do not dare to confess their sins.
r: 16. Si pœnitens númerum, et spécies, et circumstántias peccatórum explicátu necessárias non expressérit, eum Sacérdos prudénter intérroget. || 16. If the penitent has not stated the number, kinds, and circumstances of his sins which must necessarily be declared, the priest should question him prudently.
r: 17. Sed cáveat, ne curiósis, aut inutílibus interrogatiónibus quemquam detíneat, præsértim imprudénter intérrogans junióres utriúsque sexus, vel álios, de eo, quod ignórant, ne scándalum patiántur, indéque peccáre discant. || 17. But let him beware of detaining anyone with curious or useless questions, especially of imprudently questioning the young of either sex, or others, about what they do not know, lest they be scandalized and thereby learn to sin.
r: 18. Demum, audíta confessióne, perpéndens peccatórum, quæ ille admísit, magnitúdinem, ac multitúdinem, pro eórum gravitáte, ac pœniténtis conditióne, opportúnas correptiónes ac monitiónes, prout opus esse víderit, patérna caritáte adhibébit, et ad dolórem et contritiónem efficácibus verbis addúcere conábitur, atque ad vitam emendándam ac mélius instituéndam indúcet, remediáque peccatórum tradet. || 18. Finally, the confession having been heard, weighing the greatness and the number of the sins the penitent has committed, he shall, according to their gravity and the condition of the penitent, apply with fatherly charity such fitting reproofs and admonitions as he sees to be needed; he shall endeavor by effective words to bring him to sorrow and contrition, lead him to amend his life and order it better, and give him remedies against sin.
r: 19. Postrémo salutárem et conveniéntem satisfactiónem, quantum spíritus et prudéntia suggésserint, injúngat, hábita ratióne status, condiciónis, sexus, et ætátis et item dispositiónis pœniténtium. Videátque, ne pro peccátis grávibus levíssimas pœniténtias impónat, ne si forte peccátis conníveat, alienórum peccatórum párticeps efficiátur. Id vero ante óculos hábeat, ut satisfáctio non sit tantum ad novæ vitæ remédium, et infirmitátis medicaméntum, sed étiam ad præteritórum peccatórum castigatiónem. || 19. Lastly, let him enjoin a salutary and suitable satisfaction, as far as the spirit and prudence suggest, taking account of the state, condition, sex, and age, and likewise the disposition, of the penitents. And let him take care not to impose very light penances for grave sins, lest, if he should connive at sins, he become a sharer in the sins of others. Let him keep this before his eyes: that satisfaction should serve not only as a safeguard of the new life and a medicine for weakness, but also as a chastisement for past sins.
r: 20. Quare curet, quantum fíeri potest, ut contrárias peccátis pœniténtias injúngat, véluti aváris eleemósynas, libidinósis jejúnia, vel álias carnis afflictiónes, supérbis humilitátis offícia, desidiósis devotiónis stúdia. Rárius autem vel sérius confiténtibus vel in peccáta fácile recidéntibus, utilíssimum erit consúlere, ut sæpe, puta semel in mense, vel certis diébus sollémnibus, confiteántur, et si expédiat, commúnicent. || 20. Therefore let him take care, as far as possible, to enjoin penances contrary to the sins: for instance, almsgiving to the greedy, fasting or other mortifications of the flesh to the lustful, works of humility to the proud, exercises of devotion to the slothful. To those who confess seldom or late, or who easily fall back into sin, it will be most useful to advise that they confess often, say once a month or on certain solemn days, and, if it is expedient, receive Communion.
r: 21. Pœniténtias pecuniárias sibi ipsis Confessárii ne applícent, neve a pœniténtibus quidquam tamquam ministérii sui prǽmium petant, vel accípiant. || 21. Confessors are not to apply monetary penances to themselves, nor are they to ask or accept anything from penitents as a reward for their ministry.
r: 22. Pro peccátis occúltis, quantumvis grávibus, maniféstam pœniténtiam ne impónant. || 22. For hidden sins, however grave, they are not to impose a public penance.
r: 23. Vídeat autem diligénter Sacérdos, quando, et quibus conferénda, vel deneganda, vel differénda sit absolútio, ne absólvat eos, qui talis benefícii sunt incapáces: quales sunt qui nulla dant signa dolóris; qui ódia et inimicítias depónere, aut aliéna, si possunt, restitúere, aut próximam peccándi occasiónem desérere, aut álio modo peccáta derelínquere, et vitam in mélius emendáre nolunt: aut qui públicum scándalum dedérunt, nisi públice satisfáciant, et scándalum tollant: neve étiam eos absólvat, quorum peccáta sunt Superióribus reserváta. || 23. Let the priest consider carefully when and to whom absolution is to be given, refused, or deferred, lest he absolve those who are incapable of such a benefit: such as those who give no sign of sorrow; who are unwilling to lay aside hatreds and enmities, or to restore what belongs to others if they can, or to forsake the proximate occasion of sin, or in some other way to abandon their sins and amend their lives for the better; or who have given public scandal, unless they make public satisfaction and remove the scandal. Nor is he to absolve those whose sins are reserved to Superiors.
r: 24. Si vero quis confiteátur in perículo mortis constitútus, absolvéndus est ab ómnibus peccátis et censúris, quantumvis reservátis (cessat enim tunc omnis reservátio): sed prius, si potest, cui debet, satisfáciat: ac si perículum eváserit, et áliqua ratióne Superióri, a quo álias esset absolvéndus, se sístere teneátur; cum primum póterit, coram eo se sistat, quidquid debet præstitúrus. || 24. But if someone in danger of death confesses, he is to be absolved from all sins and censures, however reserved (for then all reservation ceases); but first, if he can, he should make satisfaction to whom he owes it; and if he escapes the danger and is for some reason bound to present himself to the Superior by whom he would otherwise have had to be absolved, he is to present himself to him as soon as he can, ready to do whatever he must.
r: 25. Quod si inter confiténdum, vel étiam ántequam incípiat confitéri, vox et loquéla ægro defíciant, nútibus et signis conétur, quoad ejus fíeri póterit, peccáta pœniténtis cognóscere, quibus utcúmque vel in génere, vel in spécie cógnitis, vel étiam si confiténdi desidérium sive per se, sive per álios osténderit, absolvéndus est. || 25. If, while confessing, or even before he begins to confess, the sick person loses his voice and speech, the priest should try, as far as possible, to learn the penitent's sins by nods and signs; and once these are known in any way, whether in general or in particular, or even if the penitent has shown the desire to confess, either himself or through others, he is to be absolved.
r: 26. Memínerit porro Sacérdos, ægris non esse injungéndam gravem, aut laboriósam pœniténtiam; sed indicéndam tantum illam, quam, si convalúerint, opportúno témpore péragant. Intérim juxta gravitátem morbi, áliqua oratióne, aut levi satisfactióne impósita, et acceptáta, absolvántur, prout opus erit. || 26. Let the priest remember, moreover, that no heavy or laborious penance is to be enjoined on the sick, but only such as they may perform at a suitable time if they recover. Meanwhile, according to the gravity of the illness, some prayer or light satisfaction having been imposed and accepted, they are to be absolved as need requires.

# absolution-common-form | Absolutiónis Forma Commúnis | The Common Form of Absolution | Sacraments | Tit. III, cap. 2
r: 1. Cum Sacérdos pœniténtem absólvere velit, injúncta ei prius, et ab eo acceptáta salutári pœniténtia, primo dicit: || 1. When the priest wishes to absolve the penitent, having first enjoined on him a salutary penance which the penitent has accepted, he first says:
t: Misereátur tui omnípotens Deus, et dimíssis peccátis tuis, perdúcat te ad vitam ætérnam. Amen. || May almighty God have mercy on you, forgive you your sins, and bring you to life everlasting. Amen.
r: 2. Deínde déxtera versus pœniténtem eleváta, dicit: || 2. Then, with his right hand raised toward the penitent, he says:
t: Indulgéntiam, absolutiónem, et remissiónem peccatórum tuórum tríbuat tibi omnípotens, et miséricors Dóminus. Amen. || May the almighty and merciful Lord grant you pardon, absolution, and remission of your sins. Amen.
t: Dóminus noster Jesus Christus te absólvat: et ego auctoritáte ipsíus te absólvo ab omni vínculo excommunicatiónis, suspensiónis, et interdícti, in quantum possum, et tu índiges. Deínde ego te absólvo a peccátis tuis, in nómine Patris, et Fílii, ✠ et Spíritus Sancti. Amen. || May our Lord Jesus Christ absolve you; and by His authority I absolve you from every bond of excommunication, suspension, and interdict, so far as I am able and you have need. And then I absolve you from your sins, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, ✠ and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.
r: 3. Si pœnitens sit láicus, omíttitur verbum *suspensiónis*. Epíscopus autem in absolvéndis fidélibus ter signum crucis facit. || 3. If the penitent is a layman, the word *suspension* is omitted. A bishop, however, in absolving the faithful, makes the sign of the cross three times.
t: Pássio Dómini nostri Jesu Christi, mérita beátæ Maríæ Vírginis, et ómnium Sanctórum, quidquid boni féceris, et mali sustinúeris, sint tibi in remissiónem peccatórum, augméntum grátiæ, et prǽmium vitæ ætérnæ. Amen. || May the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, the merits of the blessed Virgin Mary and of all the Saints, whatever good you do and whatever evil you endure, be for you unto the remission of sins, the increase of grace, and the reward of everlasting life. Amen.
r: 4. Justa de causa omítti potest *Misereátur, etc.*, et satis est dícere: *Dóminus noster Jesus Christus, etc.* ut supra, usque ad illud: *Pássio Dómini nostri, etc.* || 4. For a just reason the *May almighty God have mercy, etc.* may be omitted, and it is enough to say: *May our Lord Jesus Christ, etc.*, as above, up to the words: *May the Passion of our Lord, etc.*
r: 5. Urgénte vero áliqua gravi necessitáte in perículo mortis, Sacérdos bréviter dícere póterit: || 5. When some grave necessity presses in danger of death, the priest may say briefly:
t: Ego te absólvo ab ómnibus censúris, et peccátis, in nómine Patris, et Fílii, ✠ et Spíritus Sancti. Amen. || I absolve you from all censures and sins, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, ✠ and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

# absolution-excommunication | De Modo Absolvéndi ab Excommunicatióne extra Sacramentálem Confessiónem | Absolving from Excommunication outside Sacramental Confession | Sacraments | Tit. III, cap. 3
r: 1. Si potéstas absolvéndi ab excommunicatiónis senténtia Sacerdóti commíssa fúerit a Superióre, et in mandáto certa forma sit præscrípta, illa omníno servánda est: si vero in mandáto seu commissióne dicátur: *In forma Ecclésiæ consuéta absólvat*; hæc servánda sunt. || 1. If the power of absolving from a sentence of excommunication has been committed to a priest by a Superior, and a definite form is prescribed in the mandate, that form must be kept exactly; but if the mandate or commission says: *Let him absolve in the customary form of the Church*, the following is to be observed.
r: 2. Primo, ut excommunicátus ei, ob cujus offénsam in excommunicatiónem incúrrit, prius, si potest, satisfáciat. Quod si tunc non possit, sufficiéntem cautiónem præbeat, aut saltem, si eam præstáre nequeat, juret se, cum primum póterit, satisfactúrum. || 2. First, that the excommunicated person, if he can, should first make satisfaction to the one by whose injury he incurred the excommunication. If he cannot do so at that time, he should give sufficient security, or at least, if he cannot furnish that, he should swear that he will make satisfaction as soon as he can.
r: 3. Secúndo, si crimen, ob quod in excommunicatiónem incídit, sit grave, juraméntum ab eo exigátur de paréndo mandátis Ecclésiæ, quæ illi fient pro tali causa: ac præcípue ne deínceps delínquat contra illum Canónem, vel Decrétum, contra quod faciéndo censúram incúrrit. || 3. Second, if the crime for which he incurred excommunication is grave, an oath is to be required of him that he will obey the commands of the Church which will be given him for such a cause, and especially that he will not again offend against that Canon or Decree by acting against which he incurred the censure.
r: 4. Dénique hunc absolvéndi ritum observábit. Pœniténtem coram se utróque genu flexo, in húmero (si vir sit) usque ad camísiam exclusíve denudáto, virga aut funículis sedens léviter pércutit, dicéndo: || 4. Finally he shall observe this rite of absolving. The penitent kneeling on both knees before him, with the shoulder (if a man) bared down to the shirt exclusively, the priest, seated, strikes him lightly with a rod or cords, saying:

r: 5. Deínde surgit, et apérto cápite dicit: || 5. Then he rises and, with head uncovered, says:
t: Kyrie, eléison. Christe, eléison. Kyrie, eléison. || Lord, have mercy. Christ, have mercy. Lord, have mercy.
t: Pater noster *(secreto usque ad)* || Our Father *(silently until)*
v: Et ne nos indúcas in tentatiónem. || And lead us not into temptation.
x: Sed líbera nos a malo. || But deliver us from evil.
v: Salvum (-am) fac servum tuum (ancíllam tuam), Dómine. || Save Your servant (handmaid), O Lord,
x: Deus meus, sperántem in te. || O my God, who hopes in You.
v: Nihil profíciat inimícus in eo (ea). || Let the enemy have no power over him (her),
x: Et fílius iniquitátis non appónat nocére ei. || Nor the son of iniquity draw near to harm him (her).
v: Esto ei, Dómine, turris fortitúdinis. || Be unto him (her), O Lord, a tower of strength

v: Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam. || O Lord, hear my prayer.
x: Et clamor meus ad te véniat. || And let my cry come unto You.
v: Dóminus vobíscum. || The Lord be with you.
x: Et cum spíritu tuo. || And with your spirit.

# eucharist-rubrics | De Sanctíssimo Eucharístiæ Sacraménto | The Most Holy Sacrament of the Eucharist | Sacraments | Tit. IV, cap. 1
r: 1. Omnibus quidem Ecclésiæ cathólicæ Sacraméntis religióse, sanctéque tractándis, magna ac díligens cura adhibénda est: sed præcípue in administrándo, ac suscipiéndo sanctíssimo Eucharístiæ Sacraménto, quo nihil dígnius, nihil sánctius et admirabílius habet Ecclésia Dei; cum in eo contineátur præcípuum et máximum Dei donum, et ipsémet omnis grátiæ et sanctitátis fons, auctórque Christus Dóminus. || 1. Great and diligent care must indeed be taken in handling religiously and holily all the Sacraments of the Catholic Church; but especially in administering and receiving the most holy Sacrament of the Eucharist, than which the Church of God has nothing more worthy, nothing more holy and wonderful, since it contains the chief and greatest gift of God, and Christ the Lord Himself, the fount and author of all grace and holiness.
r: 2. Parochus ígitur summum stúdium in eo ponat, ut cum ipse venerábile hoc Sacraméntum, qua decet reveréntia, debitóque cultu tractet, custódiat, et administret; tum étiam pópulus sibi commíssus religióse colat, sancte frequentérque suscípiat, præsértim in majóribus anni sollemnitátibus. || 2. The parish priest, therefore, should apply the greatest zeal to this: that he himself handle, guard, and administer this venerable Sacrament with fitting reverence and due worship; and also that the people entrusted to him honor it devoutly and receive it holily and frequently, especially on the greater solemnities of the year.
r: 3. Ideo pópulum sǽpius admonébit, qua præparatióne, et quanta ánimi religióne ac pietáte, et húmili étiam córporis hábitu ad tam divínum Sacraméntum débeat accédere: ut, præmíssa sacramentáli confessióne, omnes saltem a média nocte jejúni, et utróque genu flexo Sacraméntum humíliter adórent, ac reverénter suscípiant, viri, quantum fíeri potest, a muliéribus separáti. || 3. Therefore he shall often admonish the people with what preparation, with how much devotion and piety of soul, and with what humble bearing of body as well, they ought to approach so divine a Sacrament: namely, that after sacramental confession all, fasting at least from midnight and kneeling on both knees, should humbly adore the Sacrament and reverently receive it, the men, as far as possible, separated from the women.
r: 4. Moneántur prætérea communicántes, ut, sumpto Sacraménto, non statim ab ecclésia discédant, aut colloquántur, nec statim vagis óculis circumspíciant, aut exspuant, neque de libro statim oratiónes récitent, ne Sacraménti spécies de ore decídant: sed, qua par est devotióne, aliquántisper in oratióne permáneant, grátias agéntes Deo de tam singulári benefício, atque étiam de sanctíssima passióne domínica, in cujus memóriam hoc mystérium celebrátur et súmitur. || 4. Communicants are moreover to be admonished that, having received the Sacrament, they should not at once leave the church or talk, nor immediately look about with wandering eyes, or spit, nor at once recite prayers from a book, lest the species of the Sacrament fall from their mouth; but that they should remain a while in prayer with fitting devotion, giving thanks to God for so singular a benefit, and also for the most holy Passion of the Lord, in memory of which this mystery is celebrated and received.
r: 5. Curáre porro debet, ut partículæ consecrátæ, eo número qui infirmórum et aliórum fidélium communióni satis esse possit, perpétuo conservéntur in pyxide ex sólida decentíque matéria, eáque munda, et suo opérculo bene clausa, coopérta albo velo sérico, et, quantum res feret, ornáto in tabernáculo inamovíbili in média parte altáris pósito et clave obseráto. || 5. He must further see to it that consecrated particles, in such number as may suffice for the communion of the sick and of the other faithful, are always reserved in a pyx of solid and becoming material, kept clean and well closed with its own lid, covered with a white silk veil, and placed in an immovable tabernacle, adorned as far as means allow, set in the middle of the altar and locked with a key.
r: 6. Hoc autem tabernáculum conopǽo decénter opértum, atque ab omni ália re vácuum, in Altári majóri vel in álio, quod venerationi et cultui tanti Sacraménti commódius ac decéntius videátur, sit collocátum; ita ut nullum áliis sacris functiónibus, aut ecclesiásticis offíciis impediméntum afferátur. Coram eo una saltem lampas diu noctúque continénter lúceat, nutriénda óleo olivárum vel cera apum; ubi vero óleum olivárum habéri nequeat, Ordinárii loci prudéntiæ permíttitur ut áliis óleis commutétur, quantum fíeri potest, vegetabílibus; curabítque Parochus, ut ómnia ad ipsíus Sacraménti cultum ordináta, íntegra, mundáque sint, et conservéntur. || 6. This tabernacle, becomingly covered with a canopy and empty of every other thing, is to be placed on the high altar or on another which seems more convenient and more fitting for the veneration and worship of so great a Sacrament, so that no hindrance is caused to other sacred functions or ecclesiastical offices. Before it at least one lamp is to burn continually day and night, fed with olive oil or beeswax; where olive oil cannot be had, it is left to the prudence of the local Ordinary to allow other oils, vegetable oils as far as possible, to be substituted. And the parish priest shall see that all things ordered to the worship of the Sacrament are kept whole and clean.
r: 7. Sanctíssimæ Eucharístiæ partículas frequénter renovábit. Hóstiæ vero seu partículæ consecrándæ sint recéntes; et ubi eas consecráverit, véteres primo distríbuat, vel sumat. || 7. He shall renew the particles of the most holy Eucharist frequently. The hosts or particles to be consecrated are to be fresh; and when he has consecrated them, he is first to distribute or consume the old ones.
r: 8. Fidéles omnes ad sacram communiónem admitténdi sunt, excéptis iis, qui justa ratióne prohibéntur. Arcéndi autem sunt públice indígni, quales sunt excommunicáti, interdícti, manifestóque infámes, nisi de eórum pœniténtia et emendatióne constet, et público scándalo prius satisfécerint. || 8. All the faithful are to be admitted to Holy Communion, except those who are forbidden for a just reason. Those who are publicly unworthy are to be kept away, such as the excommunicated, the interdicted, and the manifestly infamous, unless their repentance and amendment are established and they have first made satisfaction for the public scandal.
r: 9. Occúltos vero peccatóres, si occúlte petant et eos non emendátos agnóverit, miníster repéllat; non autem, si públice petant et sine scándalo ipsos præteríre nequeat. || 9. Secret sinners, if they ask secretly and the minister knows them to be unamended, he is to refuse; but not if they ask publicly and he cannot pass them over without scandal.
r: 10. Améntibus prætérea, seu phrenéticis communicáre non licet; licébit tamen, si quando hábeant lúcida intervalla, et devotiónem osténdant, dum in eo statu manent, si nullum indignitátis perículum adsit. || 10. Moreover, it is not lawful to give Communion to the insane or the deranged; it will be lawful, however, if at times they have lucid intervals and show devotion, while they remain in that state, provided there is no danger of irreverence.
r: 11. Púeris, qui propter ætátis imbecillitátem nondum hujus Sacraménti cognitiónem et gustum habent, Eucharístia ne ministrétur. || 11. The Eucharist is not to be given to children who, because of the weakness of their age, do not yet have knowledge and appreciation of this Sacrament.
r: 12. Miníster ordinárius sacræ communiónis est solus Sacérdos. Extraordinárius est Diáconus, de Ordinárii loci vel Parochi licéntia, gravi de causa concedénda, quæ in casu necessitátis legítime præsúmitur. || 12. The ordinary minister of Holy Communion is the priest alone. The extraordinary minister is the deacon, with the permission of the local Ordinary or of the parish priest, to be granted for a grave reason, and which in a case of necessity is lawfully presumed.
r: 13. Quílibet Sacérdos intra Missam et, si privátim célebrat, étiam próxime ante et statim post, sacram communiónem ministráre potest, salvo præscrípto rubricæ n. 18. Etiam extra Missam quílibet Sacérdos eádem facultáte pollet ex licéntia saltem præsúmpta Rectóris ecclésiæ, si sit extráneus. || 13. Any priest may give Holy Communion during Mass and, if he celebrates privately, also immediately before and immediately after it, without prejudice to rubric no. 18. Outside Mass, too, any priest has the same faculty, with at least the presumed permission of the rector of the church if he is a stranger there.
r: 14. Sacérdos sacram communiónem distríbuat ázymo pane vel fermentáto, secúndum próprium ritum. Ubi vero necéssitas úrgeat nec Sacérdos divérsi ritus adsit, licet Sacerdóti orientáli, qui fermentáto útitur, ministráre Eucharístiam in ázymo, vicíssim latíno aut orientáli qui útitur ázymo, ministráre in fermentáto; at suum quisque ritum ministrándi serváre debet. || 14. The priest is to distribute Holy Communion in unleavened or leavened bread according to his own rite. But where necessity presses and no priest of the other rite is present, an Eastern priest who uses leavened bread may give the Eucharist in unleavened bread, and in turn a Latin or Eastern priest who uses unleavened bread may give it in leavened; but each must keep his own rite of administering.
r: 15. Omnibus fidélibus cujusvis ritus datur fácultas ut, pietátis causa, sacraméntum Eucharísticum quólibet ritu conféctum suscípiant. Suadéndum tamen ut suo quisque ritu fidéles præcépto communiónis paschális satisfáciant. Sanctum Viáticum moribúndis ritu próprio accipiéndum est; sed, urgénte necessitáte, fas esto quólibet ritu illud accípere. || 15. To all the faithful of any rite the faculty is given, for the sake of devotion, to receive the Eucharistic Sacrament consecrated in any rite. Yet the faithful are to be urged to satisfy the precept of Easter Communion each in his own rite. Holy Viaticum is to be received by the dying in their own rite; but, when necessity presses, it is permitted to receive it in any rite.
r: 16. Omnibus diébus licet sanctíssimam Eucharístiam distribúere. Féria tamen VI Majóris Hebdómadæ solum licet sacrum Viáticum ad infírmos deférre. In Sábbato Sancto sacra commúnio nequit fidélibus ministrári nisi inter Missárum sollémnia vel contínuo ac statim ab iis explétis. Sacra commúnio iis tantum horis distribuátur, quibus Missæ sacrifícium offérri potest, nisi áliud rationábilis causa suádeat. Sacrum tamen Viáticum quacúmque diéi aut noctis hora ministrári potest. || 16. The most holy Eucharist may be distributed on all days. On Friday of Holy Week, however, it is lawful only to bring holy Viaticum to the sick. On Holy Saturday Holy Communion may not be given to the faithful except during the solemnities of the Mass or continuously and immediately after them. Holy Communion is to be distributed only at those hours at which the Sacrifice of the Mass may be offered, unless a reasonable cause suggests otherwise. Holy Viaticum, however, may be administered at any hour of the day or night.
r: 17. Sacerdóti celebránti non licet Eucharístiam intra Missam distribúere fidélibus adeo distántibus ut ipse altáre e conspéctu amíttat. || 17. A celebrating priest may not distribute the Eucharist during Mass to faithful so far off that he loses sight of the altar.
r: 18. Sacra commúnio distríbui potest ubicúmque Missam celebráre licet, étiam in oratório priváto, nisi loci Ordinárius, justis de causis, in cásibus particuláribus id prohibúerit. || 18. Holy Communion may be distributed wherever it is lawful to celebrate Mass, even in a private oratory, unless the local Ordinary, for just reasons, has forbidden it in particular cases.

# communion-order | Ordo Administrándi Sacram Communiónem | Order of Administering Holy Communion | Sacraments | Tit. IV, cap. 2
r: 1. Sacérdos sanctíssimam Eucharístiam extra Missam ministratúrus, hóstiis seu partículis pro pópuli multitúdine consecrátis, vasculóque uno vel plúribus, decénti et cómmodo loco, expósitis, cum vino et aqua ad purificatiónem eórum, qui communiónem sumpsérint, et ante eos línteo mundo exténso, lotis prius mánibus, et superpellíceo indútus, ac désuper stola colóris semper albi vel Offício illíus diéi conveniéntis (mutáto tamen colóre nigro in violáceum die Commemoratiónis Omnium Fidélium Defunctórum), præcedénte Clérico, seu álio minístro, procédit ad Altáre mánibus junctis vel bursam cum corporáli ante pectus déferens, et, accénsis céreis, facta prius genufléxione in plano, tabernáculum áperit, genufléctit, éxtrahit pyxidem, et illam super corporále depósitam discoopérit. Miníster génibus flexis nómine pópuli ad cornu Epístolæ facit confessiónem generálem, dicens: *Confíteor Deo omnipoténti, etc.* || 1. The priest who is to administer the most holy Eucharist outside Mass, the hosts or particles having been consecrated in proportion to the number of the people, and one or more small vessels having been set out in a becoming and convenient place, with wine and water for the purification of those who have received Communion, and a clean cloth spread before them; having first washed his hands and put on a surplice and, over it, a stole always of white or of the color proper to the Office of the day (black, however, being changed to violet on the day of the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed); preceded by a cleric or other minister, goes to the altar with hands joined, or carrying the burse with the corporal before his breast; and, the candles having been lit, after first genuflecting on the floor, he opens the tabernacle, genuflects, takes out the pyx, places it on the corporal and uncovers it. The minister, kneeling at the Epistle side, makes the general confession in the name of the people, saying: *I confess to almighty God, etc.*
r: 2. Tum Sacérdos íterum genufléctit, et, mánibus junctis ante pectus, vertit se ad pópulum (advértens ne terga vertat Sacraménto), et in cornu Evangélii dicit: || 2. Then the priest genuflects again and, with hands joined before his breast, turns toward the people (taking care not to turn his back to the Sacrament), and at the Gospel side says:
t: Misereátur vestri omnípotens Deus, et dimíssis peccátis vestris, perdúcat vos ad vitam ætérnam. ℟. Amen. || May almighty God have mercy on you, forgive you your sins, and bring you to life everlasting. ℟. Amen.
r: Et addit: || And he adds:
t: Indulgéntiam, absolutiónem, ✠ et remissiónem peccatórum vestrórum tríbuat vobis omnípotens, et miséricors Dóminus. ℟. Amen. || May the almighty and merciful Lord grant you pardon, absolution, ✠ and remission of your sins. ℟. Amen.
r: Dicens: *Indulgéntiam, etc.*, manu déxtera in formam crucis signat communicándos. || While saying *May the almighty and merciful Lord grant you pardon, etc.*, he signs those about to communicate with his right hand in the form of a cross.
r: 3. Deínde ad Altáre se convértit, genufléctit, manu sinístra pyxidem prehéndit; déxtera vero sumit unam partículam, quam inter póllicem et índicem tenet aliquántulum elevátam super pyxidem, conversúsque ad pópulum in médio Altáris dicit clara voce: || 3. Then he turns to the altar, genuflects, takes the pyx in his left hand, and with his right takes one particle, which he holds between thumb and forefinger, raised a little above the pyx; and, turning to the people in the middle of the altar, he says in a clear voice:
t: Ecce Agnus Dei, ecce, qui tollit peccáta mundi. || Behold the Lamb of God, behold Him who takes away the sins of the world.
r: Mox subdit: || Then he adds:
t: Dómine, non sum dignus, ut intres sub tectum meum, sed tantum dic verbo, et sanábitur ánima mea; *quod íterum, ac tértio répetit: qua formula étiam uténdum est, cum féminæ commúnio administrátur.* || Lord, I am not worthy that You should enter under my roof, but only say the word, and my soul shall be healed; *which he repeats a second and a third time; this formula is to be used also when Communion is given to a woman.*
r: 4. Póstea ad communicándum accédit, incípiens ab iis qui sunt ad partem Epístolæ; sed primo, si Sacerdótibus, vel áliis ex Clero danda sit commúnio, iis ad gradus Altáris genufléxis præbeátur, vel, si cómmode fíeri possit, intra sepiméntum Altáris sint a láicis distíncti. Sacerdótes vero et Diáconi communicántes utántur stola colóris albi vel ejúsdem colóris ac Sacérdos qui minístrat. || 4. Then he goes to give Communion, beginning with those on the Epistle side; but first, if Communion is to be given to priests or others of the clergy, it is given to them kneeling at the steps of the altar, or, if this can conveniently be done, they are to be within the altar rail, apart from the laity. Priests and deacons receiving Communion are to wear a stole of white or of the same color as that of the priest who administers.
r: 5. Sacérdos unicuíque pórrigens Sacraméntum, et fáciens cum eo signum crucis super pyxidem, simul dicit: || 5. The priest, offering the Sacrament to each one and making the sign of the cross with it over the pyx, says at the same time:
t: Corpus Dómini nostri Jesu Christi custódiat ánimam tuam in vitam ætérnam. Amen. || May the Body of our Lord Jesus Christ preserve your soul unto life everlasting. Amen.
r: 6. Ubi vero omnes communicáverint, Sacérdos, revérsus ad Altáre, pyxidem super corporále depónit, genufléctit, ac deínde dicit: || 6. When all have received Communion, the priest, returning to the altar, places the pyx on the corporal, genuflects, and then says:
t: O sacrum convívium, in quo Christus súmitur, recólitur memória passiónis ejus, mens implétur grátia, et futúræ glóriæ nobis pignus datur. *Tempore Paschali et per Octávam Córporis Christi additur:* Allelúja. || O sacred banquet, in which Christ is received, the memory of His Passion is renewed, the mind is filled with grace, and a pledge of future glory is given to us. *In Paschaltide and through the Octave of Corpus Christi there is added:* Alleluia.
v: Panem de cælo præstitísti eis. *Tempore Paschali et per Octávam Córporis Christi additur:* Allelúja. || You have given them bread from heaven. *In Paschaltide and through the Octave of Corpus Christi there is added:* Alleluia.
x: *Miníster respóndet:* Omne delectaméntum in se habéntem. *Tempore Paschali et per Octávam Córporis Christi additur:* Allelúja. || *The minister answers:* Having in it every delight. *In Paschaltide and through the Octave of Corpus Christi there is added:* Alleluia.
r: 7. Mox Sacérdos dicit: || 7. Then the priest says:
v: Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam. || O Lord, hear my prayer.
x: Et clamor meus ad te véniat. || And let my cry come unto You.
v: Dóminus vobíscum. || The Lord be with you.
x: Et cum spíritu tuo. || And with your spirit.
t: Orémus. || Let us pray.
t: Deus, qui nobis sub Sacraménto mirábili passiónis tuæ memóriam reliquísti: tríbue, quǽsumus; ita nos Córporis et Sánguinis tui sacra mystéria venerári, ut redemptiónis tuæ fructum in nobis júgiter sentiámus: Qui vivis et regnas cum Deo Patre in unitáte Spíritus Sancti Deus, per ómnia sǽcula sæculórum. ℟. Amen. || O God, who under a wonderful Sacrament have left us a memorial of Your Passion, grant, we beseech You, that we may so venerate the sacred mysteries of Your Body and Blood as ever to feel within us the fruit of Your redemption: who live and reign with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. ℟. Amen.
r: Tempore Paschali dícitur Orátio: || In Paschaltide the following prayer is said:
t: Spíritum nobis, Dómine, tuæ caritátis infúnde: ut, quos Sacraméntis paschálibus satiásti, tua fácias pietáte concórdes. Per Dóminum nostrum Jesum Christum, Fílium tuum, qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte ejúsdem Spíritus, etc. ℟. Amen. || Pour forth upon us, O Lord, the Spirit of Your love, that those whom You have filled with the Paschal Sacraments may by Your loving kindness be of one heart. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, Your Son, who lives and reigns with You in the unity of the same Holy Spirit, etc. ℟. Amen.
r: 8. Intérim, ántequam repónat Sacraméntum, diligénter advértat, ut, si áliquod fragméntum dígitis adhǽserit, illud in pyxidem depónat, et súbinde in vase cum aqua paráto eósdem dígitos, quibus tétigit Sacraméntum, ábluat, et abstérgat purificatório: aqua vero ablutiónis suo témpore mittátur in sacrárium, vel, si hoc desit, in ignem. Póstea repónit pyxidem in tabernáculo, genufléctit, et ipsíus tabernáculi ostíolum clave obsérat. || 8. Meanwhile, before he puts back the Sacrament, he is to take careful note that, if any fragment has stuck to his fingers, he puts it into the pyx; and then he washes the fingers with which he touched the Sacrament in the vessel of water prepared, and dries them with the purificator; the water of the ablution is in due time to be poured into the sacrarium, or, if there is none, into the fire. Afterwards he puts the pyx back in the tabernacle, genuflects, and locks the door of the tabernacle with the key.
r: 9. Deínde, elevátis óculis, exténdens, élevans et jungens manus caputque Cruci inclínans dicit: || 9. Then, raising his eyes, extending, raising, and joining his hands, and bowing his head to the cross, he says:
t: Benedíctio Dei omnipoténtis, *et versus ad pópulum semel tantum benedícens prosequitur* Patris, et Fílii, ✠ et Spíritus Sancti, descéndat super vos, et máneat semper. ℟. Amen. || May the blessing of almighty God, *and, turning to the people and blessing once only, he continues* the Father, and the Son, ✠ and the Holy Spirit, come down upon you and remain for ever. ℟. Amen.
r: Et ita iis, qui communicárunt, benedícit Sacérdos tam extra Missam, quam próxime ante vel statim post ipsam. || And thus the priest blesses those who have received Communion, whether outside Mass or immediately before or immediately after it.
r: 10. Ritus supérius descríptus servándus est étiam a Diácono sacram communiónem ministránte. Epíscopus autem, post sacram communiónem distribútam extra Missam, benedícit more sólito, dicens *Sit nomen Dómini benedíctum, etc.*, et ter signum crucis facit. || 10. The rite described above is to be observed also by a deacon administering Holy Communion. A bishop, however, after distributing Holy Communion outside Mass, blesses in the usual manner, saying *Blessed be the name of the Lord, etc.*, and makes the sign of the cross three times.
r: 11. Intra Missam autem commúnio pópuli statim post communiónem Sacerdótis celebrántis fíeri debet (nisi quandóque ex rationábili causa próxime ante aut statim post Missam privátam sit faciénda) cum Oratiónes, quæ in Missa post communiónem dicúntur, non solum ad Sacerdótem, sed étiam ad álios communicántes spectent. || 11. Within Mass, the Communion of the people is to take place immediately after the Communion of the celebrating priest (unless at times, for a reasonable cause, it is to be given immediately before or immediately after a private Mass), since the prayers said in the Mass after Communion concern not only the priest but also the others who communicate.
r: 12. Itaque, si qui sunt communicándi intra Missam, Sacérdos, sumpto sacratíssimo Sánguine, ántequam se puríficet, facta genufléxione, ponat partículas consecrátas in pyxide, vel, si pauci sint communicándi, super patenam, nisi a princípio pósitæ fúerint in pyxide seu álio cálice. Intérim miníster ante eos exténdit línteum seu velum album, et pro eis facit confessiónem, dicens *Confíteor Deo, etc.* Tum Sacérdos íterum genufléctit, et mánibus junctis convérsus ad pópulum in cornu Evangélii dicit: *Misereátur vestri, etc.*, et, eo quo supra dictum est modo, pórrigit communicándis Eucharístiam, incípiens a minístris Altáris, si velint communicáre. Finíta communióne, revértitur ad Altáre, nihil dicens, neque dat eis benedictiónem quia illam dabit in fine Missæ. Deínde dicit secréto: *Quod ore súmpsimus, etc.*, ut in Missáli, se puríficat, et Missam absólvit. || 12. Accordingly, if there are any to receive Communion within Mass, the priest, having consumed the most sacred Blood and before purifying himself, genuflects and places the consecrated particles in the pyx, or, if only a few are to communicate, on the paten, unless they were placed in a pyx or other chalice from the beginning. Meanwhile the minister spreads a white cloth or veil before them and makes the confession for them, saying *I confess to God, etc.* Then the priest genuflects again and, with hands joined, turned toward the people at the Gospel side, says: *May almighty God have mercy on you, etc.*, and, in the manner described above, gives the Eucharist to those who are to communicate, beginning with the ministers of the altar if they wish to receive. When Communion is finished he returns to the altar, saying nothing, nor does he give them a blessing, since he will give it at the end of Mass. Then he says silently: *What we have taken with our mouth, etc.*, as in the Missal, purifies himself, and completes the Mass.
r: 13. Quod si contíngat, próxime ante aut statim post Missam privátam, áliquos intérdum communicáre, tunc Sacérdos, planéta indútus, sacram communiónem ministrábit eo modo quo fit extra Missam, ut supra dictum est; omíssis tamen semper *Allelúja* et benedictióne in fine, si paraménta nigri colóris adhibeántur. || 13. If it happens at times that some receive Communion immediately before or immediately after a private Mass, the priest, vested in the chasuble, shall administer Holy Communion in the manner used outside Mass, as described above; always omitting, however, the *Alleluia* and the blessing at the end, if black vestments are being used.

# easter-communion | De Communióne Paschali | Easter Communion | Sacraments | Tit. IV, cap. 3
r: 1. Curet Parochus, ut in Quadragésima per se, vel per álios concionatóres pópulo opportúne denuntiéntur cánones 906 et 859, § 1, Códicis Juris Canónici, quibus declarátur: Omnis utriúsque sexus fidélis, postquam ad annos discretiónis, idest ad ratiónis usum, pervénerit, tenétur ómnia peccáta sua saltem semel in anno fidéliter confitéri, et debet semel in anno, saltem in Paschate, Eucharístiæ Sacraméntum recípere, nisi forte de consílio própríi Sacerdótis, ob áliquam rationábilem causam, ad tempus ab ejus perceptióne dúxerit abstinéndum. || 1. The parish priest is to see that during Lent, either personally or through other preachers, canons 906 and 859, § 1, of the Code of Canon Law are suitably proclaimed to the people, in which it is declared: Every one of the faithful of either sex, after reaching the years of discretion, that is, the use of reason, is bound to confess all his sins faithfully at least once a year, and must receive the Sacrament of the Eucharist at least once a year, at least at Easter, unless perhaps, on the advice of his own priest, for some reasonable cause, he judges that he should abstain from receiving it for a time.
r: 2. Paschális commúnio fiat a Domínica Palmárum ad Domínicam in Albis; sed locórum Ordináriis fas est, si ita personárum ac locórum adjúncta exígant, hoc tempus étiam pro ómnibus suis fidélibus anticipáre, non tamen ante quartam diem Domínicam Quadragésimæ, vel prorogáre, non tamen ultra Festum sanctíssimæ Trinitátis. Suadéndum fidélibus ut huic præcépto satisfáciant in sua quisque parœcia; et qui in aliéna parœcia satisfécerint, curent próprium Parochum de adimpléto præcépto certiórem fácere. Præcéptum paschális communiónis adhuc úrget, si quis illud præscrípto témpore, quávis de causa, non impléverit. || 2. Easter Communion is to be made from Palm Sunday to Low Sunday; but local Ordinaries may, if the circumstances of persons and places so require, anticipate this period for all their faithful, though not before the fourth Sunday of Lent, or extend it, though not beyond the Feast of the Most Holy Trinity. The faithful are to be urged to satisfy this precept each in his own parish; and those who have satisfied it in another parish should take care to inform their own parish priest that the precept has been fulfilled. The precept of Easter Communion still binds if anyone, for whatever reason, has not fulfilled it in the prescribed time.
r: 3. Obligátio præcépti communiónis suméndæ, quæ impúberes gravat, in eos quoque ac præcípue récidit, qui ipsórum curam habére debent, idest in paréntes, tutóres, confessárium, institutóres et Parochum. || 3. The obligation of the precept of receiving Communion which binds those below the age of puberty falls also, and especially, upon those who are bound to have care of them, that is, upon parents, guardians, confessor, teachers, and the parish priest.
r: 4. Dabit quoque óperam Parochus, quoad ejus fíeri potest, ut in ipso die sanctíssimo Paschæ fidéles commúnicent; quo die ipse per se, nisi legítime impediátur, parœciæ suæ fidélibus hoc Sacraméntum ministrábit. In céteris vero servábit ea, quæ in libro de statu animárum, ut infra, præscribúntur. || 4. The parish priest shall also strive, as far as he can, that the faithful receive Communion on the most holy day of Easter itself; on that day he himself, unless lawfully hindered, shall administer this Sacrament to the faithful of his parish. In other matters he shall observe what is prescribed below concerning the book of the state of souls.
r: 5. Ægrótis quoque parœciálibus, étiamsi communiónem extra præscríptos paschális dies sumpsérint, in paschálibus diébus illam déferet, ac ministrábit. || 5. To the sick of the parish also, even if they have received Communion outside the prescribed Easter days, he shall bring and administer it during the Easter season.

// Communion of the Sick (Viaticum)
// ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# communion-sick | De Communióne Infirmórum | Communion of the Sick (Viaticum) | The Sick | Tit. IV, cap. 4
r: 1. In perículo mortis, quávis ex causa procédat, fidéles sacræ communiónis recipiéndæ præcépto tenéntur. Etiámsi eádem die sacra communióne fúerint refécti, valde tamen suadéndum, ut in vitæ discrímen addúcti dénuo commúnicent. Perduránte mortis perículo, sanctum Viáticum, secúndum prudens confessárii consílium, plúries, distínctis diébus, administráre et licet et decet. || 1. In danger of death, from whatever cause it arises, the faithful are bound by the precept of receiving Holy Communion. Even if they have been refreshed with Holy Communion on the same day, it is nevertheless strongly to be urged that, when brought into peril of life, they communicate again. While the danger of death lasts, it is both lawful and fitting, on the prudent advice of the confessor, to administer holy Viaticum several times, on different days.
r: 2. Sanctum Viáticum infírmis ne nímium differátur; et qui animárum curam gerunt, sédulo advígilent ut eo infírmi plene sui cómpotes reficiántur. Cavéndum autem in primis est, ne ad indígnos cum aliórum scándalo deferátur, nisi sese prius sacra confessióne purgáverint, et públicæ offensióni, prout de jure, satisfécerint. || 2. Holy Viaticum is not to be put off too long for the sick; and those who have the care of souls should watch carefully that the sick are refreshed with it while in full possession of their faculties. But it must above all be avoided that it be brought, to the scandal of others, to the unworthy, unless they have first cleansed themselves by holy confession and have made satisfaction for the public offense as the law requires.
r: 3. Hortétur Parochus infírmum, ut sacram communiónem sumat, étiam si gráviter non ægrótet, aut mortis perículum non ímmineat, máxime si Festi alicújus celébritas id suádeat, neque ipse illam ministráre recusábit. || 3. The parish priest should encourage the sick person to receive Holy Communion even if he is not gravely ill and danger of death is not imminent, especially if the celebration of some feast suggests it; nor shall he himself refuse to administer it.
r: 4. Potest quidem Viáticum brevi moritúris dari non jejúnis; id tamen diligénter curándum est, ne iis tribuátur, a quibus ob phrenésim, sive ob assíduam tussim, aliúmve símilem morbum, áliqua indecéntia cum injúria tanti Sacraménti timéri potest. Céteris autem infírmis, qui ob devotiónem in ægritúdine commúnicant, danda est Eucharístia ante omnem cibum et potum, non áliter ac céteris fidélibus, quibus nec étiam per modum medicínæ ante áliquid súmere licet. Infírmi tamen qui jam a mense decúmbunt sine certa spe ut cito convaléscant, de prudénti confessárii consílio sanctíssimam Eucharístiam súmere possunt semel aut bis in hebdómada, etsi áliquam medicínam vel áliquid per modum potus ántea sumpsérint. || 4. Viaticum may indeed be given to those who are shortly to die without their being fasting; but diligent care must be taken that it is not given to those from whom, because of delirium, or constant coughing, or some other similar ailment, some irreverence with injury to so great a Sacrament may be feared. To other sick persons, however, who communicate out of devotion during their illness, the Eucharist is to be given before all food and drink, just as to the rest of the faithful, who may not take anything beforehand even by way of medicine. Yet sick persons who have been bedridden for a month without sure hope of a speedy recovery may, on the prudent advice of the confessor, receive the most holy Eucharist once or twice a week, even if they have previously taken some medicine or something by way of drink.
r: 5. Sed álicui ad adorándum solum, vel, devotiónis seu cujúsvis rei prætéxtu, ad ostendéndum ne deferátur. || 5. But it is not to be carried to anyone merely to be adored, or to be shown on the pretext of devotion or of anything else.
r: 6. Deférri autem debet hoc sanctum Sacraméntum ab ecclésia ad privátas ægrotántium domos decénti hábitu, superpósito mundo velámine, públice atque honorífice, ante pectus cum reveréntia et timóre, semper lúmine præcedénte. Privátim vero seu occúlte ad infírmos sacra commúnio ne deferátur, nisi justa et rationábilis causa áliud suádeat. || 6. This holy Sacrament is to be carried from the church to the private houses of the sick in becoming attire, covered with a clean veil, publicly and with honor, before the breast with reverence and fear, a light always going before. Holy Communion is not to be carried to the sick privately or secretly unless a just and reasonable cause suggests otherwise.
r: 7. Jus et offícium sacram communiónem públice ad infírmos étiam non parœciános extra ecclésiam deferéndi, pértinet ad Parochum intra suum territórium. Céteri Sacerdótes id possunt in casu tantum necessitátis aut de licéntia saltem præsúmpta ejúsdem Parochi vel Ordinárii. || 7. The right and duty of carrying Holy Communion publicly outside the church to the sick, even those not of his parish, belongs to the parish priest within his own territory. Other priests may do so only in case of necessity or with at least the presumed permission of the same parish priest or of the Ordinary.
r: 8. Communiónem privátim ad infírmos quílibet Sacérdos deférre potest, de vénia saltem præsúmpta Sacerdótis, cui custódia sanctíssimi Sacraménti commíssa est. Quando privátim sacra commúnio infírmis ministrátur, reveréntiæ ac decéntiæ tanto Sacraménto débitæ sédulo consulátur. || 8. Any priest may carry Communion privately to the sick, with at least the presumed permission of the priest to whom the custody of the most holy Sacrament is entrusted. When Holy Communion is administered privately to the sick, care must be taken to show the reverence and decorum due to so great a Sacrament.
r: 9. Sacram communiónem per modum Viátici sive públice sive privátim ad infírmos deférre, pértinet ad Parochum loci, in quo degunt infírmi. || 9. To carry Holy Communion as Viaticum to the sick, whether publicly or privately, belongs to the parish priest of the place where the sick are living.
r: 10. Parochus ígitur processúrus ad communicándum infírmum, áliquot campánæ íctibus júbeat convocári parochiános, seu confraternitátem Sanctíssimi Sacraménti (ubi fúerit institúta) seu álios pios Christifidéles, qui sacram Eucharístiam cum céreis, seu intortíciis comiténtur, et umbéllam, seu baldachínum, ubi habéri potest, déferant. Præmóneat, ut ægri cubículum mundétur, et in eo parétur mensa línteo mundo coopérta, in quo Sanctíssimum Sacraméntum decénter deponátur. || 10. The parish priest, then, when about to go to give Communion to a sick person, should have the parishioners, or the Confraternity of the Most Blessed Sacrament (where it has been established), or other devout faithful, summoned by several strokes of the bell, to accompany the Holy Eucharist with candles or torches and to carry the canopy or baldachin where one can be had. He should give notice beforehand that the sick person's room be cleaned and that a table be prepared in it, covered with a clean cloth, on which the Most Blessed Sacrament may be becomingly placed.
r: 11. Paréntur luminária, ac duo vascula, álterum cum vino, álterum cum aqua. Prætérea línteum mundum ante pectus communicándi ponátur, atque ália ad ornátum loci, pro cujúsque facultáte. || 11. Lights are to be prepared, and two small vessels, one with wine and the other with water. Moreover, a clean cloth is to be placed before the breast of the one who is to communicate, and other things for adorning the place, according to each one's means.
r: 12. Ubi vero convénerint qui Eucharístiam comitatúri sunt, Sacérdos indútus superpellíceo et stola, vel étiam pluviáli albi colóris, acólythis, seu Clericis, aut étiam Presbýteris (si locus feret) superpellíceo páriter indútis comitátus, decénter, et de more accéptas áliquot partículas consecrátas, vel unam tantum (si longius aut difficílius iter sit faciéndum) ponat in pyxide, seu parva custódia, quam próprio suo opérculo coopérit, et velum séricum superimpónit: ipse vero Sacérdos, impósito sibi prius ab utróque húmero oblóngo velo decénti, utráque manu accípiat vas cum Sacraménto, et deínde umbéllam, seu baldachínum súbeat, nudo cápite processúrus. || 12. When those who are to accompany the Eucharist have assembled, the priest, vested in surplice and stole, or also in a white cope, and accompanied by acolytes or clerics, or even by priests (if the place allows), likewise vested in surplices, having becomingly and in the customary manner taken several consecrated particles, or only one (if the journey is to be rather long or difficult), places them in a pyx or small custodial, which he covers with its own lid, and over it he places a silk veil. The priest himself, having first put on a becoming oblong veil over both shoulders, takes the vessel with the Sacrament in both hands and then goes under the canopy or baldachin, to proceed bareheaded.
r: 13. Præcédat semper acólythus, vel álius miníster déferens latérnam (noctu autem hoc Sacraméntum deférri non debet, nisi necéssitas úrgeat); sequántur duo Clérici, vel qui illórum vices súppleant; quorum alter aquam benedíctam cum aspersório, et bursam cum corporáli, quod supponéndum erit vásculo Sanctíssimi Sacraménti super mensa in cubículo infírmi, et cum linteolo purificatório ad dígitos Sacerdótis abstergéndos; alter hunc librum Rituálem déferat, et campánulam júgiter pulset. Succédant deínde deferéntes intortícia. Postrémo Sacérdos Sacraméntum gestans elevátum ante pectus sub umbélla, dicens Psalmum *Miserére*, et álios Psalmos, et Cántica. Quod si lóngius, aut difficílius iter obeúndum sit, et fortásse étiam equitándum, necésse erit vas, in quo Sacraméntum défertur, bursa decénter ornáta, et ad collum appénsa, apte inclúdere, et ita ad pectus alligáre, atque obstríngere, ut neque decídere, neque pyxide excúti Sacraméntum queat. || 13. An acolyte or other minister carrying a lantern always goes in front (but this Sacrament ought not to be carried at night unless necessity requires); two clerics follow, or those who take their place: one of them carries holy water with the sprinkler, and the burse with the corporal, which is to be placed under the vessel of the Most Blessed Sacrament on the table in the sick person's room, together with the purificator for drying the priest's fingers; the other carries this book of the Ritual and rings the small bell continually. Then follow those carrying torches. Last comes the priest, bearing the Sacrament raised before his breast under the canopy, saying the Psalm *Have mercy* and other psalms and canticles. If the journey to be made is rather long or difficult, and perhaps even on horseback, it will be necessary to enclose the vessel in which the Sacrament is carried securely in a becomingly adorned burse hung from the neck, and so to bind and fasten it to the breast that the Sacrament can neither fall nor be shaken out of the pyx.
r: 14. Ingrédiens vero locum, ubi jacet infírmus, dicit: || 14. On entering the place where the sick person lies, he says:
v: Pax huic dómui. || Peace to this house.
x: Et ómnibus habitántibus in ea. || And to all who dwell therein.
r: 15. Tum depósitum Sacraméntum super mensa, suppósito corporáli, genufléxus adórat, ómnibus in genua procumbéntibus, et velum humerále dimíttit; mox accépta aqua benedícta, aspérgit infírmum, et cubículum, dicens Antíphonam: || 15. Then, having placed the Sacrament on the table with the corporal beneath it, he adores on bended knee, all kneeling down, and he lays aside the humeral veil; then, taking holy water, he sprinkles the sick person and the room, saying the antiphon:
t: *Ant.* Aspérges me, Dómine, hyssópo, et mundábor: lavábis me, et super nivem dealbábor. || *Ant.* Sprinkle me, O Lord, with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed; wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow.
r: Et primum versum Psalmi *Miserére*, cum *Glória Patri, etc. Sicut erat, etc.* || And the first verse of the Psalm *Have mercy*, with *Glory be, etc. As it was, etc.*
t: *Ps. 50.* Miserére mei, Deus, * secúndum magnam misericórdiam tuam. || *Ps. 50.* Have mercy on me, O God, * according to Your great mercy.

v: Adjutórium nostrum in nómine Dómini. || Our help is in the name of the Lord.
x: Qui fecit cælum et terram. || Who made heaven and earth.
v: Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam. || O Lord, hear my prayer.
x: Et clamor meus ad te véniat. || And let my cry come unto You.
v: Dóminus vobíscum. || The Lord be with you.
x: Et cum spíritu tuo. || And with your spirit.
t: Orémus. || Let us pray.
t: Exáudi nos, Dómine sancte, Pater omnípotens, ætérne Deus: et míttere dignéris sanctum Angelum tuum de cælis, qui custódiat, fóveat, prótegat, vísitet, atque deféndat omnes habitántes in hoc habitáculo. Per Christum, Dóminum nostrum. ℟. Amen. || Hear us, holy Lord, almighty Father, eternal God, and be pleased to send Your holy Angel from heaven to guard, cherish, protect, visit, and defend all who dwell in this dwelling. Through Christ our Lord. ℟. Amen.
r: 16. His dictis, accédit ad infírmum, ut cognóscat, num sit bene dispósitus ad suscipiéndum sacrum Viáticum, et utrum velit áliqua peccáta confitéri; et illum áudiat, atque absólvat: quamvis prius debéret esse rite conféssus, nisi necéssitas áliter úrgeat. || 16. These things said, he goes to the sick person to find out whether he is well disposed to receive holy Viaticum and whether he wishes to confess any sins; and he hears him and absolves him, although he ought to have confessed properly beforehand, unless necessity presses otherwise.
r: 17. Póstea facta de more confessióne generáli, sive ab infírmo, sive ejus nómine ab álio, Sacérdos dicit in singulári: || 17. Afterwards, the general confession having been made in the usual way, either by the sick person or by another in his name, the priest says in the singular:
t: Misereátur tui .... etc. Indulgéntiam .... tuórum tríbuat tibi etc. || May almighty God have mercy on you ... etc. May the almighty and merciful Lord grant you pardon ... of your sins, etc.
r: Si plúribus simul infírmis in eódem cubículo vel loco Sacraméntum administrétur, Sacérdos dicat in plurali: *Misereátur vestri, etc., Indulgéntiam ... vestrórum tríbuat vobis, etc.* || If the Sacrament is administered to several sick persons together in the same room or place, the priest says in the plural: *May almighty God have mercy on you all, etc., May the almighty and merciful Lord grant you all pardon ... of your sins, etc.*
r: 18. Deínde, facta genufléxione, áccipit Sacraméntum de vásculo, atque illud élevans osténdit infírmo, dicens: || 18. Then, having genuflected, he takes the Sacrament from the vessel and, raising it, shows it to the sick person, saying:
t: Ecce Agnus Dei, ecce, qui tollit peccáta mundi. || Behold the Lamb of God, behold Him who takes away the sins of the world.
r: Et more sólito ter dicit: || And in the usual way he says three times:
t: Dómine, non sum dignus, ut intres sub tectum meum, sed tantum dic verbo, et sanábitur ánima mea. || Lord, I am not worthy that You should enter under my roof, but only say the word, and my soul shall be healed.
r: 19. Et infírmus, simul cum Sacerdóte, dicit éadem verba, saltem semel, submíssa voce. Tum Sacérdos dans infírmo Eucharístiam, dicit: || 19. And the sick person, together with the priest, says the same words, at least once, in a low voice. Then the priest, giving the Eucharist to the sick person, says:
t: Accipe, frater (soror), Viáticum Córporis Dómini nostri Jesu Christi, qui te custódiat ab hoste malígno, et perdúcat in vitam ætérnam. Amen. || Receive, brother (sister), the Viaticum of the Body of our Lord Jesus Christ, that He may guard you from the wicked enemy and bring you to life everlasting. Amen.
r: 20. Si vero commúnio non datur per modum Viátici, dicat more ordinário: || 20. If, however, Communion is not given as Viaticum, he says in the ordinary way:
t: Corpus Dómini nostri Jesu Christi custódiat ánimam tuam in vitam ætérnam. Amen. || May the Body of our Lord Jesus Christ preserve your soul unto life everlasting. Amen.
r: 21. Quod si mors ímmineat, et perículum sit in mora, tunc dicto *Misereátur, etc.* prædíctis précibus ómnibus, vel ex parte, omíssis, ei statim Viáticum præbeátur. || 21. If death is imminent and there is danger in delay, then, after the *May almighty God have mercy, etc.* has been said, all or some of the aforesaid prayers being omitted, Viaticum is to be given him at once.
r: 22. Póstea Sacérdos ábluit dígitos in vase cum aqua paráto, nihil dicens, et abstérgit purificatório; aqua vero ablutiónis suo témpore míttitur in sacrárium, vel, si hoc desit, in ignem. Deínde dicit: || 22. Afterwards the priest washes his fingers in the vessel of water prepared, saying nothing, and dries them with the purificator; the water of the ablution is in due time poured into the sacrarium, or, if there is none, into the fire. Then he says:
v: Dóminus vobíscum. || The Lord be with you.
x: Et cum spíritu tuo. || And with your spirit.
t: Orémus. || Let us pray.
t: Dómine sancte, Pater omnípotens, ætérne Deus, te fidéliter deprecámur, ut accipiénti fratri nostro (soróri nostræ) sacrosánctum Corpus Dómini nostri Jesu Christi, Fílii tui, tam córpori, quam ánimæ prosit ad remédium sempitérnum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte Spíritus Sancti Deus, per ómnia sǽcula sæculórum. ℟. Amen. || Holy Lord, almighty Father, eternal God, we faithfully implore You that the most sacred Body of our Lord Jesus Christ, Your Son, may profit our brother (sister) who has received it, as an everlasting remedy for body as well as soul: who lives and reigns with You in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. ℟. Amen.
r: Si plúribus simul infírmis in eódem cubículo vel loco Eucharístia præbeátur, Sacérdos dicat in plurali: *Dómine sancte, ..., ut accipiéntibus frátribus (soróribus) nostris, etc.* || If the Eucharist is given to several sick persons together in the same room or place, the priest says in the plural: *Holy Lord, ..., that ... our brothers (sisters) who have received, etc.*
r: 23. His explétis, si áltera partícula Sacraménti superfúerit (superésse autem semper debet, præterquam in casu jam dicto), velum humerále reassúmit, genufléctit, surgit, et cum Sacraménto in pyxide velo humeráli coopérta facit signum crucis super infírmum nihil dicens. Tunc reverénter illud déferens, órdine quo vénerat, revértitur ad ecclésiam, dicéndo Psalmum *Laudáte Dóminum de cælis, etc.*, et álios Psalmos et Hymnos, prout tempus feret. || 23. When these things are completed, if another particle of the Sacrament remains (and one ought always to remain, except in the case already mentioned), he takes up the humeral veil again, genuflects, rises, and with the Sacrament in the pyx covered by the humeral veil makes the sign of the cross over the sick person, saying nothing. Then, carrying it reverently, he returns to the church in the order in which he came, saying the Psalm *Praise the Lord from the heavens, etc.*, and other psalms and hymns as time allows.
r: 24. Cum pervénerit ad ecclésiam, ponit Sacraméntum super Altáre, genufléctit, ac deínde dicit: || 24. When he has reached the church, he places the Sacrament on the altar, genuflects, and then says:
v: Panem de cælo præstitísti eis. *Tempore Paschali et per Octávam Córporis Christi additur:* Allelúja. || You have given them bread from heaven. *In Paschaltide and through the Octave of Corpus Christi there is added:* Alleluia.
x: Omne delectaméntum in se habéntem. *Tempore Paschali et per Octávam Córporis Christi additur:* Allelúja. || Having in it every delight. *In Paschaltide and through the Octave of Corpus Christi there is added:* Alleluia.
v: Dóminus vobíscum. || The Lord be with you.
x: Et cum spíritu tuo. || And with your spirit.
t: Orémus. || Let us pray.
t: Deus, qui nobis sub Sacraménto mirábili passiónis tuæ memóriam reliquísti: tríbue, quǽsumus; ita nos Córporis et Sánguinis tui sacra mystéria venerári, ut redemptiónis tuæ fructum in nobis júgiter sentiámus: Qui vivis et regnas in sǽcula sæculórum. ℟. Amen. || O God, who under a wonderful Sacrament have left us a memorial of Your Passion, grant, we beseech You, that we may so venerate the sacred mysteries of Your Body and Blood as ever to feel within us the fruit of Your redemption: who live and reign for ever and ever. ℟. Amen.
r: 25. Deínde annúntiat Indulgéntias a Summis Pontifícibus concéssas Sanctíssimum Sacraméntum comitántibus. || 25. Then he announces the indulgences granted by the Supreme Pontiffs to those who accompany the Most Blessed Sacrament.
r: 26. Póstea cum Sacraménto in pyxide velo humeráli coopérta facit signum crucis super pópulum, nihil dicens. Postrémo illud in loco suo repónit. || 26. Afterwards, with the Sacrament in the pyx covered by the humeral veil, he makes the sign of the cross over the people, saying nothing. Lastly he puts it back in its place.
r: 27. Quod si, ob difficultátem aut longitúdinem itíneris, vel quia, ea qua decet veneratióne, Sacraméntum ad ecclésiam cómmode reportári non posset, sumpta fúerit una tantum partícula consecráta, ut dictum est, tunc ea infírmo administráta, Sacérdos, prædíctis précibus recitátis, eum manu benedícit, dicens more sólito: *Benedíctio Dei omnipoténtis ... descéndat super te (vel vos, si plures infírmos communicáverit) etc.* et una cum áliis priváto hábitu, exstínctis lumínibus, umbélla demíssa, laténte pyxide, ad ecclésiam, vel domum quisque suam revértitur. || 27. If, because of the difficulty or length of the journey, or because the Sacrament could not conveniently be brought back to the church with fitting veneration, only one consecrated particle was taken, as was said, then, having administered it to the sick person and recited the aforesaid prayers, the priest blesses him with his hand, saying in the usual manner: *May the blessing of almighty God ... come down upon you (or upon you all, if he has given Communion to several sick persons), etc.*; and together with the others, in ordinary dress, the lights extinguished, the canopy lowered, and the pyx concealed, each returns to the church or to his own home.
r: 28. Ritus supérius descríptus servándus est étiam a Diácono sacram communiónem ministránti. || 28. The rite described above is to be observed also by a deacon administering Holy Communion.
r: 29. Quando, ex justa et rationábili causa, privátim sacra commúnio ad infírmos défertur, Sacérdos saltem stolam semper hábeat própriis coopértam véstibus; in sácculo seu bursa pyxidem recóndat, quam per funículos collo appénsam in sinu repónat; et numquam solus procédat, sed uno saltem fidéli, in deféctu Clérici, associétur. Cum autem ad infírmi cubículum pervénerit, Sacérdos superpellíceum quoque índuat cum stola, si illud ántea non indúerit. || 29. When, for a just and reasonable cause, Holy Communion is carried privately to the sick, the priest is always to wear at least a stole, covered by his own clothing; he is to enclose the pyx in a small bag or burse, which, hung from his neck by cords, he places in his bosom; and he is never to go alone, but is to be accompanied by at least one of the faithful, in the absence of a cleric. When he has reached the sick person's room, the priest is also to put on a surplice with the stole, if he has not put it on before.
