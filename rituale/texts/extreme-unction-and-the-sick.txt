# extreme-unction-rules | De Sacramento Extremæ Unctionis | On the Sacrament of Extreme Unction | Sacraments | Tit. V, cap. 1
r: Extrémæ Unctionis Sacramentum a Christo Domino institutum, tamquam cælestis medicina, non animæ solum, sed etiam corpori salutaris, quamvis per se non sit de necessitate medii ad salutem, nemini tamen licet neglegere, et omni studio ac diligentia periculose ægrotantibus adhibendum est, et eo quidem tempore, si fieri possit, cum illis adhuc integra mens et ratio viget, ut ad uberiorem Sacramenti gratiam percipiendam, ipsi etiam suam fidem, ac piam animi voluntatem conferre possint, dum sacro liniuntur Oleo. || The Sacrament of Extreme Unction, instituted by Christ the Lord as a heavenly medicine profitable not only to the soul but also to the body, although not of itself necessary for salvation as a means, may nevertheless be neglected by no one; it is to be administered with all zeal and care to those who are dangerously ill, and indeed, if possible, at a time when their mind and reason are still sound, so that, in order to receive the more abundant grace of the Sacrament, they themselves may also bring to it their faith and the devout will of their soul while they are being anointed with the holy Oil.
r: 2. In quo illud in primis ex generali Ecclesiæ consuetudine observandum est, ut, si tempus, et infirmi condicio permittat, ante Extremam Unctionem, Pœnitentiæ et Eucharistiæ Sacramenta infirmis præbeantur. || 2. In this matter, according to the general custom of the Church, it is above all to be observed that, if time and the condition of the sick person permit, the Sacraments of Penance and the Eucharist be given to the sick before Extreme Unction.
r: 3. Habeat igitur Parochus in ecclesia loco nitido et decenter ornato, in vase argenteo seu stanneo, sub clavi diligenter custoditum sacrum Oleum infirmorum, quod singulis annis, Feria V in Cena Domini ab Episcopo benedictum, veteri combusto, renovandum est; neque adhibeatur vetus, nisi necessitas urgeat. Mox deficienti Oleo benedicto aliud oleum de olivis non benedictum adjiciatur, etiam iterato, minore tamen copia. || 3. The parish priest, therefore, is to keep in the church, in a clean and becomingly adorned place, in a vessel of silver or pewter, carefully guarded under lock and key, the holy Oil of the sick. It is to be renewed every year with the Oil blessed by the bishop on Holy Thursday, the old being burned; nor is the old to be used unless necessity presses. If the blessed Oil is running short, other unblessed olive oil may be added, even repeatedly, but always in a smaller quantity.
r: 4. Parochus Oleum sacrum a suo Ordinario petere debet; nec illud domi retineat, nisi propter necessitatem aliamve rationabilem causam, accedente Ordinarii licentia. || 4. The parish priest must obtain the holy Oil from his own Ordinary; and he is not to keep it at home except out of necessity or for some other reasonable cause, with the permission of the Ordinary.
r: 5. Oleum porro ipsum vel per se solum, vel in bombacio seu re simili servari potest; sed ad evitandum effusionis periculum multo commodius ad infirmos defertur in bombacio. || 5. The Oil itself may be kept either by itself alone or in cotton wool or something similar; but to avoid the danger of spilling, it is much more conveniently carried to the sick in cotton wool.
r: 6. Hoc sacramentum valide administrat omnis et solus Sacerdos. || 6. Every priest, and only a priest, validly administers this sacrament.
r: Minister ordinarius est Parochus loci, in quo degit infirmus; in casu autem necessitatis, vel de licentia saltem rationabiliter præsumpta ejusdem Parochi vel Ordinarii loci, alius quilibet Sacerdos hoc sacramentum ministrare potest. || The ordinary minister is the parish priest of the place where the sick person is staying; but in case of necessity, or with the permission, at least reasonably presumed, of that parish priest or of the local Ordinary, any other priest may administer this sacrament.
r: 7. Minister ordinarius ex justitia tenetur hoc sacramentum per se ipse vel per alium administrare, et in casu necessitatis ex caritate quilibet Sacerdos. || 7. The ordinary minister is bound in justice to administer this sacrament himself or through another; and in case of necessity any priest is bound in charity.
r: 8. Extrema Unctio præberi non potest nisi fideli, qui, post adeptum usum rationis, ob infirmitatem vel senium in periculo mortis versetur. || 8. Extreme Unction can be given only to one of the faithful who, having attained the use of reason, is in danger of death through sickness or old age.
r: In eadem infirmitate hoc sacramentum iterari non potest, nisi infirmus post susceptam Unctionem convaluerit et in aliud vitæ discrimen inciderit. || In the same illness this sacrament cannot be repeated, unless the sick person has recovered after receiving the Anointing and has fallen into another danger of life.
r: 9. Quando dubitatur num infirmus usum rationis attigerit, num in periculo mortis reipsa versetur vel num mortuus sit, hoc sacramentum ministretur sub condicione. || 9. When it is doubtful whether the sick person has attained the use of reason, whether he is really in danger of death, or whether he is already dead, this sacrament is to be administered conditionally.
r: 10. Hoc sacramentum non est conferendum illis qui impœnitentes in manifesto peccato mortali contumaciter perseverant; quod si hoc dubium fuerit, conferatur sub condicione. || 10. This sacrament is not to be conferred on those who obstinately persevere unrepentant in manifest mortal sin; if this is doubtful, it is to be conferred conditionally.
r: 11. Infirmis autem qui, cum suæ mentis compotes essent, illud saltem implicite petierunt aut verisimiliter petiissent, etiamsi deinde sensus vel usum rationis amiserint, nihilominus absolute præbeatur. || 11. But to the sick who, while they were in possession of their faculties, asked for it at least implicitly, or would probably have asked for it, it is nevertheless to be given absolutely, even if they have since lost their senses or the use of reason.
r: 12. Si quis autem laboret in extremis, et periculum immineat, ne decedat antequam finiantur Unctiones, cito ungatur, incipiendo ab eo loco: Per istam sanctam Unctiónem, etc., ut infra: deinde, si adhuc supervivat, dicantur Orationes prætermissæ, suo loco positæ. || 12. If someone is at the point of death, and there is danger that he may die before the Anointings are finished, he is to be anointed at once, beginning at the words "Through this holy Anointing," etc., as below; then, if he still survives, the prayers that were passed over are to be said, each in its proper place.
r: 13. Si vero dum inungitur infirmus decedat, Presbyter ultra non procedat, et prædictas Orationes omittat. || 13. If the sick person dies while being anointed, the priest is not to proceed further, and he omits the aforesaid prayers.
r: 14. Quod si dubitet an vivat adhuc, Unctionem prosequatur, sub condicione pronuntiando formam, dicens: Si vivis, per istam sanctam Unctiónem, etc., ut infra. || 14. But if he doubts whether the person is still alive, he continues the Anointing, pronouncing the form conditionally and saying: "If you are living, through this holy Anointing," etc., as below.
r: 15. Si autem acciderit, infirmum post peccatorum suorum confessionem ad exitum vitæ properare, tunc cum sacro Viatico poterit et Oleum infirmorum ad eum deferri per ipsum Sacerdotem, qui defert sacram Eucharistiam: si tamen alius Presbyter, vel Diaconus, qui Oleum sanctum deferat, haberi possit, per ipsum deferatur, qui superpelliceo indutus cum Oleo sacro occulte delato sequatur Sacerdotem Viaticum portantem; et postquam infirmus Viaticum sumpserit, inungatur a Sacerdote. || 15. If it happens that the sick person, after the confession of his sins, is hastening toward the end of life, then the Oil of the sick may be carried to him together with the holy Viaticum by the same priest who carries the Holy Eucharist. If, however, another priest or a deacon can be had to carry the holy Oil, it is to be carried by him; vested in a surplice, he follows the priest bearing the Viaticum, carrying the holy Oil unseen; and after the sick person has received Viaticum, he is anointed by the priest.
r: 16. Quinque vero corporis partes præcipue ungi debent, quas veluti sensuum instrumenta homini natura tribuit, nempe oculi, aures, nares, os et manus: attamen pedes etiam ungendi sunt; sed pedum unctio ex qualibet rationabili causa omitti potest. Unctiones autem, extra casum gravis necessitatis, ipsa ministri manu nulloque adhibito instrumento fiant. || 16. Five parts of the body in particular must be anointed, which nature has given to man as the instruments, so to speak, of the senses: namely the eyes, ears, nostrils, mouth, and hands. The feet are also to be anointed, but the anointing of the feet may be omitted for any reasonable cause. Except in case of grave necessity, the anointings are to be made with the minister's own hand, without the use of any instrument.
r: 17. Manus vero, quæ reliquis infirmis interius ungi debent, Presbyteris exterius ungantur. || 17. The hands, which in other sick persons are to be anointed on the inside, are anointed on the outside in the case of priests.
r: 18. Dum oculos, aures, et alia corporis membra, quæ paria sunt, Sacerdos ungit, caveat, ne alterum ipsorum inungendo, Sacramenti formam prius absolvat, quam ambo hujusmodi paria membra perunxerit. || 18. When the priest anoints the eyes, ears, and other members of the body that are paired, he must take care not to complete the form of the Sacrament while anointing only one of them, before he has anointed both members of the pair.
r: 19. Si quis autem sit aliquo membro mutilatus, pars loco illi proxima inungatur, eadem verborum forma. || 19. If someone is lacking some member, the part nearest to it is anointed, with the same form of words.
r: 20. Hujus Sacramenti forma, qua sancta Romana Ecclesia utitur, sollemnis illa precatio est, quam Sacerdos ad singulas unctiones adhibet, cum ait: || 20. The form of this Sacrament used by the holy Roman Church is that solemn prayer which the priest uses at each anointing, when he says:
t: Per istam sanctam Unctiónem, et suam piíssimam misericórdiam, indúlgeat tibi Dóminus quidquid per visum, sive per audítum, etc., deliquísti. Amen. || Through this holy Anointing and His most tender mercy, may the Lord forgive you whatever sins you have committed by sight, or by hearing, etc. Amen.
r: 21. In casu autem necessitatis sufficit unica unctio in uno sensu, seu rectius in fronte, cum hac forma breviori: || 21. In case of necessity, however, a single anointing on one sense, or more properly on the forehead, suffices, with this shorter form:
t: Per istam sanctam Unctiónem indúlgeat tibi Dóminus quidquid deliquísti. Amen. || Through this holy Anointing may the Lord forgive you whatever sins you have committed. Amen.
r: Salva tamen manet obligatio singulas unctiones supplendi, ut supra (n. 12), cessante periculo. || The obligation remains, however, of supplying the individual anointings, as above (n. 12), once the danger has passed.
r: 22. Quando pluribus simul infirmis hoc Sacramentum ministratur, Sacerdos singulis ægrotis crucem pie deosculandam porrigat, omnes preces quæ unctiones præcedunt, plurali numero semel recitet, unctiones cum respectivis formis super singulos ægrotos efficiat, omnes vero preces, quæ unctiones subsequuntur, plurali numero semel dicat. || 22. When this Sacrament is administered to several sick persons at once, the priest offers the cross to each of them to kiss devoutly, recites once, in the plural, all the prayers that precede the anointings, performs the anointings with their respective forms on each sick person individually, and says once, in the plural, all the prayers that follow the anointings.

# extreme-unction | Ordo ministrandi Sacramentum Extremæ Unctionis | Order of Administering the Sacrament of Extreme Unction | Sacraments | Tit. V, cap. 2
r: Sacerdos Sacramentum Extremæ Unctionis ministraturus, quatenus fieri poterit, parari curet apud infirmum mensam mappa candida coopertam, itemque vas, in quo sit bombacium, seu quid simile in sex globulos distinctum, ad abstergendas partes inunctas; medullam panis ad detergendos digitos; et aquam ad abluendas Sacerdotis manus; ceream item candelam quæ deinde accensa ipsi ungenti lumen præbeat. Denique operam dabit, ut quanta poterit munditia ac nitore hoc Sacramentum ministretur. || The priest who is to administer the Sacrament of Extreme Unction should see to it, as far as possible, that there be prepared beside the sick person a table covered with a white cloth; also a vessel containing cotton wool or something similar divided into six little balls, for wiping the anointed parts; crumbs of bread for cleansing the fingers; water for washing the priest's hands; and likewise a wax candle, which, when lit, will give light to him as he anoints. Finally, he will take care that this Sacrament be administered with all possible cleanliness and neatness.
r: 2. Deinde convocatis Clericis seu ministris, vel saltem uno Clerico, qui crucem sine hasta, aquam benedictam cum aspersorio, et librum Ritualem deferat, ipse Parochus decenter accipit vas sacri Olei infirmorum sacculo serico violacei coloris inclusum, illudque caute deferat, ne effundi possit. Quod si longius iter peragendum, aut etiam equitandum sit, vel alias adsit periculum effusionis, vas Olei sacculo, aut bursa inclusum, ut dictum est, ad collum appendat, ut commodius et securius perferat. Procedat autem sine sonitu campanulæ. || 2. Then, having called together the clerics or servers, or at least one cleric to carry a cross without a staff, holy water with the sprinkler, and the Ritual, the parish priest himself reverently takes the vessel of the holy Oil of the sick, enclosed in a little bag of violet silk, and carries it carefully so that it cannot be spilled. If a longer journey must be made, or even on horseback, or if there is otherwise a danger of spilling, he hangs the vessel of Oil, enclosed in its little bag or burse as was said, around his neck, so as to carry it more conveniently and safely. He goes without ringing a bell.
r: 3. Cum perventum fuerit ad locum, ubi jacet infirmus, Sacerdos intrans cubiculum, dicit: || 3. On arriving at the place where the sick person lies, the priest, entering the room, says:
v: Pax huic dómui. || Peace be to this house.
x: Et ómnibus habitántibus in ea. || And to all who dwell in it.
r: 4. Deinde deposito Oleo super mensam, superpelliceo, stolaque violacea indutus, ægroto crucem pie deosculandam porrigit; mox in modum crucis aqua benedicta eum, et cubiculum, et circumstantes aspergit, dicens Antiphonam: Aspérges me, Dómine, etc. Quod si ægrotus velit confiteri, audiat illum, et absolvat. Deinde piis verbis illum consoletur, et de hujus Sacramenti vi, atque efficacia, si tempus ferat, breviter admoneat: et quantum opus sit, ejus animam confirmet, et in spem erigat vitæ æternæ. || 4. Then, having set the Oil down on the table and being vested in surplice and violet stole, he offers the cross to the sick person to kiss devoutly. Next he sprinkles him, the room, and those standing by with holy water in the form of a cross, saying the antiphon "Sprinkle me, O Lord," etc. If the sick person wishes to confess, he hears him and absolves him. Then he consoles him with devout words and, if time allows, briefly instructs him on the power and efficacy of this Sacrament; and as far as needed he strengthens his soul and raises him up to the hope of eternal life.
r: 5. Postea dicit: || 5. Afterwards he says:
v: Adjutórium nostrum in nómine Dómini. || Our help is in the name of the Lord.
x: Qui fecit cælum et terram. || Who made heaven and earth.
v: Dóminus vobíscum. || The Lord be with you.
x: Et cum spíritu tuo. || And with your spirit.
t: Orémus. || Let us pray.
t: Intróeat, Dómine Jesu Christe, domum hanc sub nostræ humilitátis ingréssu, ætérna felícitas, divína prospéritas, seréna lætítia, cáritas fructuósa, sánitas sempitérna: effúgiat ex hoc loco accéssus dæmonum: adsint Angeli pacis, domúmque hanc déserat omnis malígna discórdia. Magnífica, Dómine, super nos nomen sanctum tuum; et béne✠dic nostræ conversatióni: sancti✠fica nostræ humilitátis ingréssum, qui sanctus et qui pius es, et pérmanes cum Patre et Spíritu Sancto in sǽcula sæculórum. ℟. Amen. || Lord Jesus Christ, as we in our lowliness enter this house, let there enter with us everlasting happiness, divine prosperity, serene gladness, fruitful charity, and lasting health. Let the approach of demons flee from this place; let the Angels of peace be present, and let all evil discord depart from this house. Magnify Your holy name upon us, O Lord; ✠ bless our coming among them, ✠ sanctify the entrance of us lowly ones; You who are holy and merciful, and abide with the Father and the Holy Spirit for ever and ever. ℟. Amen.
t: Orémus, et deprecémur Dóminum nostrum Jesum Christum, ut benedicéndo bene✠dícat hoc tabernáculum, et omnes habitántes in eo, et det eis Angelum bonum custódem, et fáciat eos sibi servíre ad considerándum mirabília de lege sua: avértat ab eis omnes contrárias potestátes: erípiat eos ab omni formídine, et ab omni perturbatióne, ac sanos in hoc tabernáculo custodíre dignétur: Qui cum Patre et Spíritu Sancto vivit et regnat Deus in sǽcula sæculórum. ℟. Amen. || Let us pray and beseech our Lord Jesus Christ that with His blessing He may ✠ bless this dwelling and all who live in it; that He may give them a good Angel as guardian, and make them serve Him by considering the wonders of His law; that He may turn away from them all hostile powers, deliver them from all fear and from all disturbance, and graciously keep them in health in this dwelling. Who with the Father and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns, God, for ever and ever. ℟. Amen.
t: Orémus. || Let us pray.
t: Exáudi nos, Dómine sancte, Pater omnípotens, ætérne Deus: et míttere dignéris sanctum Angelum tuum de cælis, qui custódiat, fóveat, prótegat, vísitet, atque deféndat omnes habitántes in hoc habitáculo. Per Christum, Dóminum nostrum. ℟. Amen. || Hear us, holy Lord, almighty Father, eternal God, and be pleased to send Your holy Angel from heaven to guard, cherish, protect, visit, and defend all who dwell in this house. Through Christ our Lord. ℟. Amen.
r: 6. Quæ Orationes, si tempus non patiatur, ex parte, vel in totum poterunt omitti. Tunc de more facta confessione generali, latino vel vulgari sermone, Sacerdos dicit in singulari numero: Misereátur tui, etc., Indulgéntiam ... tuórum tríbuat tibi, etc. || 6. If time does not allow, these prayers may be omitted in part or entirely. Then, the general confession having been made in the customary way, in Latin or in the vernacular, the priest says in the singular: "May almighty God have mercy on you," etc., and "May the almighty and merciful Lord grant you pardon ... of your sins," etc.
r: 7. Antequam Parochus incipiat ungere infirmum, moneat astantes, ut pro illo orent, et, ubi commodum sit, pro loco et tempore, et astantium numero, vel qualitate, recitent septem Psalmos Pœnitentiales cum Litaniis Sanctorum, vel alias preces, dum ipse Unctionis Sacramentum administrat. Mox, extensa manu dextera super caput infirmi, dicit: || 7. Before the parish priest begins to anoint the sick person, he exhorts those present to pray for him and, where convenient, according to the place and time and the number or kind of those present, to recite the seven Penitential Psalms with the Litany of the Saints, or other prayers, while he administers the Sacrament of Anointing. Then, extending his right hand over the head of the sick person, he says:
n: The seven Penitential Psalms and the Litany of the Saints are given under "Litany of the Saints".
t: In nómine Pa✠tris, et Fí✠lii, et Spíritus ✠ Sancti, exstinguátur in te omnis virtus diáboli per impositiónem mánuum nostrárum, et per invocatiónem gloriósæ et sanctæ Dei Genetrícis Vírginis Maríæ, ejúsque íncliti Sponsi Joseph, et ómnium sanctórum Angelórum, Archangelórum, Patriarchárum, Prophetárum, Apostolórum, Mártyrum, Confessórum, Vírginum, atque ómnium simul Sanctórum. Amen. || In the name of the ✠ Father, and of the ✠ Son, and of the Holy ✠ Spirit, may every power of the devil be extinguished in you through the laying on of our hands, and through the invocation of the glorious and holy Mother of God, the Virgin Mary, of her illustrious Spouse Joseph, and of all the holy Angels, Archangels, Patriarchs, Prophets, Apostles, Martyrs, Confessors, Virgins, and all the Saints together. Amen.
r: 8. Deinde, intincto pollice in Oleo sancto, in modum crucis ungit infirmum in partibus hic subscriptis, aptando proprio loco verba formæ in hunc modum: || 8. Then, having dipped his thumb in the holy Oil, he anoints the sick person in the form of a cross on the parts listed below, fitting the words of the form to each place in this way:
h: Ad oculos || On the eyes
t: Per istam sanctam Unctió✠nem, et suam piíssimam misericórdiam, indúlgeat tibi Dóminus quidquid per visum deliquísti. Amen. || Through this holy ✠ Anointing and His most tender mercy, may the Lord forgive you whatever sins you have committed by sight. Amen.
r: 9. Minister vero, si est in Sacris, vel ipsemet Sacerdos, post quamlibet Unctionem, tergat loca inuncta novo globulo bombacii, vel rei similis, eumque in vase mundo reponat, et ad ecclesiam postea deferat, comburat, cineresque projiciat in sacrarium. || 9. After each anointing the minister, if he is in Sacred Orders, or else the priest himself, wipes the anointed places with a fresh little ball of cotton wool or something similar, and places it in a clean vessel; afterwards he carries it to the church, burns it, and throws the ashes into the sacrarium.
h: Ad aures || On the ears
t: Per istam sanctam Unctió✠nem, et suam piíssimam misericórdiam, indúlgeat tibi Dóminus quidquid per audítum deliquísti. Amen. || Through this holy ✠ Anointing and His most tender mercy, may the Lord forgive you whatever sins you have committed by hearing. Amen.
h: Ad nares || On the nostrils
t: Per istam sanctam Unctió✠nem, et suam piíssimam misericórdiam, indúlgeat tibi Dóminus quidquid per odorátum deliquísti. Amen. || Through this holy ✠ Anointing and His most tender mercy, may the Lord forgive you whatever sins you have committed by smell. Amen.
h: Ad os, compressis labiis || On the mouth, with the lips closed
t: Per istam sanctam Unctió✠nem, et suam piíssimam misericórdiam, indúlgeat tibi Dóminus quidquid per gustum et locutiónem deliquísti. Amen. || Through this holy ✠ Anointing and His most tender mercy, may the Lord forgive you whatever sins you have committed by taste and speech. Amen.
h: Ad manus || On the hands
t: Per istam sanctam Unctió✠nem, et suam piíssimam misericórdiam, indúlgeat tibi Dóminus quidquid per tactum deliquísti. Amen. || Through this holy ✠ Anointing and His most tender mercy, may the Lord forgive you whatever sins you have committed by touch. Amen.
r: 10. Et adverte, quod Sacerdotibus, ut dictum est, manus non inunguntur interius, sed exterius. || 10. And note that, as has been said, the hands of priests are anointed not on the inside but on the outside.
h: Ad pedes || On the feet
t: Per istam sanctam Unctió✠nem, et suam piíssimam misericórdiam, indúlgeat tibi Dóminus quidquid per gressum deliquísti. Amen. || Through this holy ✠ Anointing and His most tender mercy, may the Lord forgive you whatever sins you have committed by your steps. Amen.
r: 11. Hæc autem unctio ad pedes, ut dictum est, ex qualibet rationabili causa omitti potest. || 11. This anointing of the feet, as has been said, may be omitted for any reasonable cause.
r: 12. Quibus omnibus peractis, Sacerdos pollicem fricat cum medulla panis, manus lavat linteoque abstergit; aqua vero lotionis cum pane, suo tempore, mittatur in sacrarium, vel, si hoc desit, in ignem. Deinde dicit: || 12. When all this is done, the priest rubs his thumb with the bread crumbs, washes his hands, and dries them with a towel; the water of the washing, together with the bread, is in due time to be poured into the sacrarium or, if there is none, into the fire. Then he says:
t: Kyrie, eléison. Christe, eléison. Kyrie, eléison. || Lord, have mercy. Christ, have mercy. Lord, have mercy.
t: Pater noster *(secreto usque ad)* || Our Father *(silently until)*
v: Et ne nos indúcas in tentatiónem. || And lead us not into temptation.
x: Sed líbera nos a malo. || But deliver us from evil.
v: Salvum (-am) fac servum tuum (ancíllam tuam). || Save Your servant.
x: Deus meus, sperántem in te. || Who trusts in You, my God.
v: Mitte ei, Dómine, auxílium de sancto. || Send him (her) help, O Lord, from Your holy place.
x: Et de Sion tuére eum (eam). || And from Sion watch over him (her).

# visitation-sick | De Visitatione et Cura Infirmorum | The Visitation and Care of the Sick | The Sick | Tit. V, cap. 4
r: Parochus in primis meminisse debet, non postremas esse muneris sui partes, ægrotantium curam habere. Quare cum primum noverit, quempiam ex fidelibus in sua parœcia ægrotare, non exspectabit, ut ad eum vocetur, sed ultro ad illum accedat: idque non semel tantum, sed sæpius, quatenus opus sit; horteturque parœcianos suos, ut ipsum admoneant, cum aliquem in parœcia sua ægrotare contigerit, præcipue si morbus gravior sit. || The parish priest must above all remember that the care of the sick is not the least part of his office. Therefore, as soon as he learns that any of the faithful in his parish is sick, he will not wait to be called, but will go to him of his own accord, and not once only but more often, as far as is needed; and he should urge his parishioners to inform him whenever anyone in his parish falls sick, especially if the illness is serious.
r: 2. Ad hoc juvabit, præsertim in amplis parœciis, ægrotorum notam seu catalogum habere, ut cujusque statum et condicionem cognoscat, eorumque memoriam facilius retinere, et illis opportune subvenire possit. || 2. To this end it will help, especially in large parishes, to keep a record or list of the sick, so that he may know the state and condition of each, may more easily keep them in mind, and may assist them in good time.
r: 3. Quod si Parochus, legitime impeditus, infirmorum, ut quando plures sunt, visitationi interdum vacare non possit: id præstandum curabit per alios Sacerdotes, si quos habeat in parœcia sua, aut saltem per laicos homines pios et christiana caritate præditos. || 3. If the parish priest, being legitimately hindered, cannot at times devote himself to visiting the sick, as when there are many of them, he will see that this is done by other priests, if he has any in his parish, or at least by devout lay people endowed with Christian charity.
r: 4. Ægrotos visitans, ea, qua Sacerdotes Domini decet, honestate et gravitate se habeat, ut non ægris solum, sed sibi et domesticis verbo et exemplo prosit ad salutem. || 4. In visiting the sick he is to conduct himself with the propriety and seriousness befitting priests of the Lord, so that by word and example he may further the salvation not only of the sick but of himself and of the household.
r: 5. Eorum vero præcipuam curam geret, qui, humanis auxiliis destituti, benigni ac providi Pastoris caritatem et operam requirunt. Quibus si non possit ipse succurrere de suo, et eleemosynas illis, prout debet, si facultas suppetat, erogare, quantum fieri potest, sive per caritatis, vel alterius nominis confraternitatem, si in ea civitate vel loco sit, sive per privatas, sive per publicas collectas et eleemosynas, illorum necessitatibus succurrendum curabit. || 5. He will take special care of those who, being destitute of human help, need the charity and service of a kind and provident shepherd. If he cannot himself help them from his own means, giving them alms as he ought when he has the means, he will see to it, as far as possible, that their needs are relieved, whether through a confraternity of charity or one of another name, if there is one in that city or place, or through private or public collections and alms.
r: 6. In primis autem spiritualem ægrotantium curam suscipiat, omnemque diligentiam in eo ponat, ut in viam salutis eos dirigat, atque a diabolicis insidiis salutarium adjumentorum præsidio defendat, ac tueatur. || 6. Above all, however, he should undertake the spiritual care of the sick and devote all diligence to directing them on the way of salvation, and to defending and protecting them from the snares of the devil with the safeguard of saving helps.
r: 7. Accedat autem ad ægrotum ita paratus, ut in promptu habeat argumenta ad persuadendum apta, ac præsertim Sanctorum exempla, qui plurimum valent: quibus eum in Domino consoletur, excitet, ac recreet. Horteturque, ut omnem spem suam in Deo ponat, peccatorum suorum pœniteat, divinam misericordiam imploret, et infirmitatis pœnas, tamquam paternam Dei visitationem, patienter ferat, et ad salutem suam provenisse credat, ut vitam, moresque suos melius instituat. || 7. He should come to the sick person so prepared that he has ready at hand arguments suited to persuade, and especially the examples of the Saints, which are most effective, by which he may console, rouse, and refresh him in the Lord. He should exhort him to place all his hope in God, to repent of his sins, to implore the divine mercy, and to bear patiently the sufferings of his illness as a fatherly visitation from God, believing that it has come for his salvation, so that he may better order his life and conduct.
r: 8. Deinde, qua par est prudentia et caritate, infirmum ad sacram confessionem inducat, et confitentem audiat, etiamsi velit totius vitæ peccata confiteri. || 8. Then, with due prudence and charity, he should lead the sick person to holy confession and hear him confess, even if he wishes to confess the sins of his whole life.
r: 9. Illud præterea diligenter servari curabit, ne quis pro corporali salute aliquid ægroto suadeat, vel adhibeat, quod in detrimentum animæ convertatur. || 9. He will also take careful precautions that no one, for the sake of bodily health, persuades the sick person to anything, or applies anything to him, that would turn to the harm of his soul.
r: 10. Ubi vero periculum immineat, Parochus monebit ægrotum, ne dæmonum astutia, neque medicorum pollicitationibus, neque propinquorum, aut amicorum blanditiis se ullo modo decipi sinat, quo minus ea, quæ ad animæ salutem pertinent, opportune procuret, et, qua par est devotione et celeritate, sancta Sacramenta, dum sana mens est, integrique sensus, religiose suscipiat, citra fallacem illam ac perniciosam procrastinationem, quæ plurimos ad æterna supplicia perduxit, in diesque, fallente diabolo, perducit. || 10. When danger threatens, the parish priest will warn the sick person not to let himself be deceived in any way, whether by the cunning of the demons, or by the promises of physicians, or by the flatteries of relatives or friends, so as to fail to attend in good time to what concerns the salvation of his soul; but rather, with due devotion and promptness, while his mind is sound and his senses whole, to receive the holy Sacraments religiously, without that deceitful and pernicious putting-off which has led very many to eternal punishment and, through the deceit of the devil, daily leads others there.
r: 11. Quod si æger aliquis hortationibus ac monitis Sacerdotum, vel amicorum et domesticorum consiliis adduci non possit, ut velit peccata sua confiteri, tunc non omnino desperanda res est, sed quamdiu ille vivit, repetendæ sunt frequentes variæ et efficaces Sacerdotum, et aliorum piorum hominum exhortationes; proponendaque æternæ salutis damna, et sempiternæ mortis supplicia; ostendendaque immensa Dei misericordia, eum ad pœnitentiam provocantis, ad ignoscendum paratissimi. Adhibendæ sunt etiam tum privatæ tum publicæ ad Deum preces, ad divinam gratiam impetrandam pro salute miseri decumbentis. || 11. If some sick person cannot be brought by the exhortations and admonitions of priests, or by the counsels of friends and household, to be willing to confess his sins, the matter is still not to be wholly despaired of; but as long as he lives, frequent, varied, and effective exhortations of priests and other devout persons are to be repeated. The loss of eternal salvation and the torments of everlasting death are to be set before him, and the immense mercy of God is to be shown him, who calls him to repentance and is most ready to forgive. Prayers to God, both private and public, are also to be offered to obtain divine grace for the salvation of the unhappy sufferer.
r: 12. Videbit denique Sacerdos, quibus potissimum tentationibus, aut pravis opinionibus æger sit subjectus, eique, prout opus erit, apta remedia prudenter adhibebit. || 12. Finally, the priest will observe to what temptations or false opinions the sick person is chiefly subject, and will prudently apply suitable remedies as needed.
r: 13. Sacras imagines Christi Domini crucifixi, beatæ Mariæ Virginis, et Sancti, quem æger præcipue veneratur, ob oculos ejus apponi curabit. Vasculum item adsit aquæ benedictæ, qua frequenter aspergatur. || 13. He will have sacred images of Christ the Lord crucified, of the Blessed Virgin Mary, and of the Saint to whom the sick person is especially devoted, placed before his eyes. There should also be a small vessel of holy water, with which he may frequently be sprinkled.
r: 14. Proponet etiam ægrotanti, prout ejus condicio feret, aliquas breves orationes, et pias mentis ad Deum excitationes: præsertim versiculos e Psalmorum libro, vel Orationem Dominicam, et Salutationem Angelicam, Symbolum fidei, vel passionis Domini nostri meditationem, et Sanctorum martyria et exempla, ac cælestis gloriæ beatitudinem. Hæc tamen opportune et discrete suggerantur, ne ægroto molestia, sed levamen afferatur. || 14. He will also suggest to the sick person, as his condition allows, some short prayers and devout aspirations of the mind to God: especially verses from the Book of Psalms, or the Lord's Prayer and the Angelic Salutation, the Creed, or a meditation on the Passion of our Lord, the martyrdoms and examples of the Saints, and the blessedness of heavenly glory. These things, however, are to be suggested opportunely and with discretion, so as to bring the sick person relief and not annoyance.
r: 15. Consoletur infirmum, dicens, se pro eo in Missæ sacrificio, et aliis precibus oraturum, curaturumque ut alii itidem pro eo faciant, idque re ipsa præstabit. || 15. He should console the sick person by telling him that he will pray for him in the Sacrifice of the Mass and in other prayers, and will see that others do the same for him; and he will actually do so.
r: 16. Si morbus gravior, vel cum periculo sit, ægroto suadeat, ut dum integra mente est, rem suam omnem recte constituat, et testamentum faciat; si quid habeat alienum, restituat, et ad remedium animæ suæ pro facultatibus, quod in Domino ei placuerit, disponat: sed hæc suggerendo, omnis avaritiæ nota caveatur. || 16. If the illness is serious or dangerous, he should advise the sick person, while his mind is whole, to set all his affairs in proper order and make his will; to restore anything belonging to another that he may have; and to dispose, according to his means, whatever may please him in the Lord for the good of his soul. But in suggesting these things, every appearance of avarice is to be avoided.
r: 17. Hortetur denique, ut si convaluerit, ante omnia ad ecclesiam veniat, ubi Deo gratias agat de restituta valetudine, et sacram communionem pie suscipiat; ac deinceps meliorem vitæ disciplinam teneat. || 17. Lastly, he should exhort him, if he recovers, to come first of all to the church, there to thank God for his restored health and devoutly to receive Holy Communion, and thereafter to keep a better rule of life.
r: 18. Sequentes preces omnes, vel ex parte, prout tempus, et ægrotorum condicio feret, pro arbitrio Sacerdotis dici, vel omitti possunt. || 18. The following prayers may be said in whole or in part, or omitted, at the priest's discretion, as time and the condition of the sick allow.

# helping-dying | Modus Juvandi Morientes | The Manner of Helping the Dying | The Dying & the Dead | Tit. V, cap. 5
r: Ingravescente morbo, Parochus infirmum frequentius visitabit, et ad salutem diligenter juvare non desinet: monebitque instante periculo se confestim vocari, ut in tempore præsto sit morienti, sumptoque sanctissimo Viatico, et sacra Unctione adhibita, si periculum immineat, statim commendationis animæ officium præstabit, de quo infra. Sed si tempus suppetat, sequentia pietatis officia præstare poterit, si ita expedire judicaverit, pro condicione personæ. || As the illness grows worse, the parish priest will visit the sick person more often and will not cease to help him diligently toward salvation. He will ask to be called at once when danger is at hand, so that he may be present in time for the dying person; and when the most holy Viaticum has been received and the holy Anointing given, if danger is imminent, he will at once carry out the office of the commendation of the soul, which is given below. But if time allows, he may perform the following offices of piety, if he judges it expedient, according to the condition of the person.
r: 2. Ac primo, ut ægrotus Indulgentiam legitima auctoritate concessam consequi possit, eam illi reducat ad mentem, proponatque, quid ad eam consequendam agi debeat; præsertim, ut contrito corde sanctissimum nomen JESU semel, vel sæpius invocet. || 2. And first, so that the sick person may gain the Indulgence granted by lawful authority, he should remind him of it and explain what must be done to gain it: especially that he invoke the most holy name of JESUS, once or more often, with a contrite heart.
r: 3. Deinde hortetur infirmum, et excitet, ut dum mente viget, eliciat actus fidei, spei, et caritatis, aliarumque virtutum, nempe: || 3. Then he should exhort and encourage the sick person, while his mind is still vigorous, to make acts of faith, hope, and charity, and of the other virtues, namely:
r: Ut firmiter credat omnes articulos fidei, et quidquid sancta Romana Ecclesia catholica et apostolica credit, et docet. || That he firmly believe all the articles of faith, and whatever the holy Roman Church, catholic and apostolic, believes and teaches.
r: Ut speret, Christum Dominum nostrum pro sua immensa clementia sibi fore propitium; et merito ejus sanctissimæ passionis, et per intercessionem beatæ Mariæ et omnium Sanctorum, se vitam æternam consecuturum. || That he hope that Christ our Lord, in His immense clemency, will be gracious to him, and that through the merit of His most holy Passion and by the intercession of Blessed Mary and all the Saints he will attain eternal life.
r: Ut toto corde diligat et maxime diligere cupiat Dominum Deum ea dilectione, qua illum diligunt Beati Sanctique omnes. || That he love the Lord God with his whole heart, and desire above all to love Him with that love with which all the Blessed and Saints love Him.
r: Ut ob amorem Dei doleat ex corde de omni offensa, qualitercumque contra Dominum Deum et proximum commissa. || That for the love of God he be heartily sorry for every offence, in whatever way committed, against the Lord God and his neighbor.
r: Ut ex corde ob amorem Dei parcat omnibus, qui sibi quoquo modo fuerint molesti aut inimici. || That for the love of God he forgive from his heart all who have in any way troubled him or been his enemies.
r: Ut ab iis veniam postulet, quos aliquando dictis aut factis offendit. || That he ask pardon of those whom he has at any time offended by word or deed.
r: Ut quem patitur dolorem et morbi molestiam, propter Deum in pœnitentiam peccatorum suorum patienter toleret. || That he bear patiently, for God's sake and in penance for his sins, the pain and distress of illness that he suffers.
r: Ut si Dominus sibi salutem corporis præstare dignabitur, proponat de cetero pro viribus suis a peccatis cavere, et ejus mandata servare. || That, if the Lord deigns to grant him bodily health, he resolve henceforth, as far as he is able, to guard against sin and to keep His commandments.
r: 4. Hortetur præterea, ut, eo modo quo potest, saltem ex corde, ita per intervalla precetur: || 4. He should further exhort him to pray at intervals in this way, as best he can, at least in his heart:
t: Miserére mei, Deus, secúndum magnam misericórdiam tuam. || Have mercy on me, O God, according to Your great mercy.
t: In te, Dómine, sperávi: non confúndar in ætérnum. || In You, O Lord, have I hoped; let me never be put to shame.
t: In manus tuas, Dómine, comméndo spíritum meum: redemísti me, Dómine, Deus veritátis. || Into Your hands, O Lord, I commend my spirit; You have redeemed me, O Lord, God of truth.
t: Deus, in adjutórium meum inténde: Dómine, ad adjuvándum me festína. || O God, come to my assistance; O Lord, make haste to help me.
t: Esto mihi, Dómine, in Deum protectórem. || Be to me, O Lord, a God and protector.
t: Deus, propítius esto mihi peccatóri. || O God, be merciful to me, a sinner.
t: Dulcíssime Dómine Jesu Christe, per virtútem sanctíssimæ passiónis tuæ, récipe me in númerum electórum tuórum. || Most sweet Lord Jesus Christ, by the power of Your most holy Passion, receive me into the number of Your elect.
t: Dómine Jesu Christe, súscipe spíritum meum. || Lord Jesus Christ, receive my spirit.
t: María, mater grátiæ, mater misericórdiæ, tu me ab hoste prótege, et hora mortis súscipe. || Mary, mother of grace, mother of mercy, protect me from the enemy and receive me at the hour of death.
t: Sancte Angele Dei, mihi custos assíste. || Holy Angel of God, my guardian, stand by me.
t: Omnes sancti Angeli et omnes Sancti, intercédite pro me et mihi succúrrite. || All you holy Angels and all you Saints, intercede for me and come to my aid.
r: 5. Hæc, et his similia poterit prudens Sacerdos vulgari, vel latino sermone, pro personæ captu, morienti suggerere. || 5. These and similar prayers a prudent priest may suggest to the dying person, in the vernacular or in Latin, according to the person's capacity.

# apostolic-blessing-death | Ritus Benedictionis Apostolicæ cum Indulgentia Plenaria in Articulo Mortis | Rite of the Apostolic Blessing with Plenary Indulgence at the Hour of Death | The Dying & the Dead | Tit. V, cap. 6
r: Benedictio Apostolica cum indulgentia plenaria in articulo mortis cum soleat impertiri post Sacramenta Pœnitentiæ, Eucharistiæ et Extremæ Unctionis illis infirmis, qui vel illam petierint, dum sana mente et integris sensibus erant, seu verisimiliter petiissent, vel dederint signa contritionis; impertienda iisdem est, etiam si postea linguæ, ceterorumque sensuum usu sint destituti, aut in delirium vel amentiam inciderint. Excommunicatis vero, impœnitentibus, et qui in manifesto peccato mortali moriuntur, est omnino deneganda. || The Apostolic Blessing with plenary indulgence at the hour of death is customarily given, after the Sacraments of Penance, the Eucharist, and Extreme Unction, to those sick persons who either asked for it while of sound mind and in possession of their senses, or would probably have asked for it, or who have given signs of contrition; it is to be given to them even if afterwards they have lost the use of speech and of the other senses, or have fallen into delirium or insanity. But it is to be altogether refused to the excommunicated, to the impenitent, and to those who die in manifest mortal sin.
r: 2. Parochus aliusve Sacerdos qui infirmo assistat, superpeliceo et stola violacea indutus, ingrediendo cubiculum, ubi jacet infirmus, dicat: Pax huic dómui, etc., ac deinde ægrotum, cubiculum et circumstantes aspergat aqua benedicta, dicendo Antiphonam: Aspérges me, etc. || 2. The parish priest, or another priest attending the sick person, vested in surplice and violet stole, on entering the room where the sick person lies says: "Peace be to this house," etc.; and then he sprinkles the sick person, the room, and those standing by with holy water, saying the antiphon "Sprinkle me," etc.
r: 3. Quod si ægrotus velit confiteri, audiat illum, et absolvat. Si confessionem non petat, excitet illum ad eliciendum actum contritionis; de hujus Benedictionis efficacia ac virtute, si tempus ferat, breviter admoneat; tum instruat, atque hortetur, ut sanctissimum nomen Jesu, corde saltem, invocet, morbi incommoda ac dolores in anteactæ vitæ expiationem libenter perferat; Deoque sese paratum offerat ad ultro acceptandum, quidquid ei placuerit, et mortem ipsam patienter obeundam in satisfactionem pœnarum, quas peccando promeruit. || 3. If the sick person wishes to confess, he hears him and absolves him. If he does not ask for confession, the priest should encourage him to make an act of contrition and, if time allows, briefly instruct him on the efficacy and power of this Blessing. Then he should teach and exhort him to invoke the most holy name of Jesus, at least in his heart; to bear willingly the discomforts and pains of illness in expiation of his past life; and to offer himself to God, ready to accept freely whatever may please Him, and to meet death itself patiently in satisfaction for the punishments he has deserved by sinning.
r: 4. Tum piis ipsum verbis consoletur, in spem erigens, fore, ut ex divinæ munificentiæ largitate eam pœnarum remissionem, et vitam sit consecuturus æternam. || 4. Then he should console him with devout words, raising him to the hope that, through the bounty of the divine generosity, he will obtain that remission of punishment and life everlasting.
